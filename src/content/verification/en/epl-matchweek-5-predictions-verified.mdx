Two full hits, two partial hits (the winner call missed, but the actual recommended betting angle landed), and two flat-out misses — the worst round of the season so far.

## The two hits: one right on direction but nowhere near "comfortable," one that landed despite low confidence

Man City vs Sunderland got the winner right, but this might be City's least comfortable match of the season: [Enzo Fernández](wiki:Enzo_Fernández) opened the scoring with a free-kick, Sunderland hit back fast, with [Brian Brobbey](wiki:Brian_Brobbey) equalizing within three minutes; [Cherki](wiki:Rayan_Cherki) restored City's lead in the 30th minute, but that didn't last either, with Brobbey finishing again from close range to make it 2-2; right before halftime, another City new signing, [Antoine Semenyo](wiki:Antoine_Semenyo), fired into the bottom corner to put City 3-2 up at the break. Just before the hour mark in the second half, Semenyo struck again to make it 4-2, but Brobbey immediately completed his hat-trick to pull it back to 4-3, keeping the outcome in real doubt right to the end. It took [Haaland](wiki:Erling_Haaland) finishing off a [Gvardiol](wiki:Joško_Gvardiol) break down the left with 9 minutes to go to finally settle it at 5-3. Sunderland actually posted a higher xG (4.61) than City (2.72) in this match — a genuine sign they created real danger, but City's individual quality converted a bunch of half-chances into goals when it mattered. So "comfortable" might be the wrong word for how this one played out, but the overall directional call still holds up.

Fulham vs Man United is the only match where the "how it hit" matched the pre-match read exactly — the pre-match piece explicitly flagged low confidence, saying a narrow win, a draw, or even an upset wouldn't be a surprise, and the match really did end 1-1 ([Lisandro Martínez](wiki:Lisandro_Martínez) own goal, [Matheus Cunha](wiki:Matheus_Cunha) equalizing in stoppage time). It might feel a bit like getting lucky, but it's also fair to say reading both sides' current form accurately is exactly what made this prediction land. The difficulty of predicting football, and the satisfaction when it actually works out, is a big part of what makes betting on this sport fun in the first place.

## The two partial hits: the winner call missed, but the actual recommended bet was right

The Brentford vs Chelsea conclusion never actually backed a straight Chelsea win — it explicitly said "more recommend betting Both Teams to Score or Over." What actually happened diverged a bit from that: Brentford shut Chelsea out 3-0, so BTTS didn't land, but it genuinely was an Over. And the fact that the keeper who conceded three still ended up with the highest match rating tells you everything about how absurd Chelsea's defense has been this season — they can't seem to stop anyone, always leaking goals from some angle you didn't see coming. In a situation like that, swapping which keeper starts probably just shifts you from conceding a lot to conceding slightly less — conceding at all isn't really avoidable. The bigger problem might be that the back line itself doesn't seem to know how bad it is — there was even a moment in the match where defenders were seen blaming the keeper. Unless the manager actually does something about it, betting the Over on every Chelsea match going forward is probably the move. For Brentford, this was their first home win over Chelsea in 88 years, going all the way back to 1938 — that kind of once-in-a-generation upset is exactly the variable hardest to build into any reasoning framework, but at least the Over call landed on the right side.

Bournemouth vs Liverpool is another case of missing the headline direction but nailing the fallback — the conclusion said "personally I think it's quite plausible they draw again, or it's a low-scoring match." Liverpool's attacking output really has been thin this season, and here they got their only goal of the match through [Cody Gakpo](wiki:Cody_Gakpo)'s assist for Isak down the right, with [Alisson](wiki:Alisson_Becker) also making a couple of key saves. The draw didn't land, but the total of just 1 goal matched the "low-scoring" call exactly. Liverpool also climbed back to 6th on 9 points heading into the international break with this win.

## The two misses: the Arsenal match is exactly the risk the previous piece's closing paragraph flagged

Brighton vs Arsenal is the most notable miss of the round, and arguably the worst defeat of Arsenal's season so far. Arsenal had a shot at matching a 123-year-old club record of eight straight wins to open a season, and instead got thoroughly beaten away from home: [Pascal Groß](wiki:Pascal_Groß) opened the scoring in the 31st minute, [Charalampos Kostoulas](wiki:Charalampos_Kostoulas) doubled the lead in first-half stoppage time, and Chema Andrés headed home in the 59th to seal it — Brighton actually had chances to add a fourth in the second half, so 3-0 arguably flattered Arsenal a bit. Interestingly, Arsenal actually posted a slightly higher xG (1.58) than Brighton (1.33) in this one — a near-perfect mirror of the City-Sunderland match: one side where the numbers said the other team should have won but didn't (City), and one where the numbers said Arsenal were still the better side and they still got battered (Arsenal). That's a reminder that xG is only ever a reference point — what actually decides a match is often execution and sharpness on the day, and this is honestly the weakest link in Arsenal's squad this season: their forward line is a bit short on individual quality, so their finishing tends to fall just short at the crucial moment. Last season they could paper over that with well-drilled set-piece routines, but with opponents having done their homework on those patterns this time around, how Arsenal shore up that last piece of the attack will decide whether they can sustain last season's level.

The previous pre-match piece's closing paragraph specifically noted: "no matter how good the recent form or how pretty the seven-game streak, three question marks appearing in the back line at once is a signal worth carrying into the next round's framework more than any single scoreline" — and Arsenal really did suffer their first loss of the season in this match. In the end I still judged that the squad's bench depth would hold up, but as that pre-match piece said, once Arsenal actually lose one match, that's when the real warning signs start — going forward it's not enough to just back Arsenal by default; their injuries and fatigue levels need to be scrutinized much more closely.

Good news: Spurs finally scored. Bad news: they still didn't win. Spurs vs Villa was a textbook "both sides are bad, I picked the less-bad one and still got it wrong." I did correctly call that Spurs would end their goal drought in this match, but I didn't see their defense being quite this broken coming — I probably shouldn't have expected anything from Spurs at all, and if I'm honest, some lingering fondness for Kane and Son Heung-min probably nudged my read a little. This loss also makes Spurs' start to the season (2 points, -6 goal difference) their worst ever, even worse than their 2008-09 opening (2 points, -3 goal difference) — the winner call just didn't land.

## The lesson from this round: weight injuries and fatigue more heavily

This is the lowest-hit-rate round of the season so far, but more worth remembering than the hit rate itself is the Arsenal lesson — I genuinely saw the risk in their defensive depth and still didn't let that risk actually shape my final read.
