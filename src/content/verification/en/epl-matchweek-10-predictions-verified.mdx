## The Two Hits: Arsenal and Liverpool, My Two Most Confident Picks

**Arsenal 1-0 Hull.** The pick and the "under" fallback both landed. [Ødegaard](wiki:Martin_Ødegaard) scored from close range on the right of the six-yard box on 72'; Arsenal had 21 shots (10 on target, 6 blocked) and 66.9% possession, while Hull managed only 7 shots and 2 on target, and goalkeeper Tzolakis made 9 saves to keep the game going for 72 minutes.

Before the game I wrote "Hull's style leans defensive and values tempo, but that kind of game is exactly what Arsenal are best at", and so it proved: Arsenal had the ball throughout and the shots were 21 to 7, with Hull holding out for 72 minutes thanks to nine saves from their goalkeeper. The "Arsenal's attack is on the weak side" point was borne out once again too: 21 shots for one goal. On the pre-match injuries: Saka was out (right, not in the squad); Saliba wasn't in the squad (the pre-match line was that he might make his first bench appearance); Konsa started (the pre-match report said he came off in Europe with a sore knee and wasn't serious); Calafiori started (expected back, right); and Madueke started (listed as a doubt in one preview). For Hull, Crooks wasn't in the squad (suspended, right) and Butland was an unused substitute. Arsenal are on 27 points, 5 clear of second-placed Brighton.

**Palace 1-3 Liverpool.** The pick and the "over" fallback both landed (4 goals). [Mac Allister](wiki:Alexis_Mac_Allister) headed in from [Szoboszlai](wiki:Dominik_Szoboszlai)'s corner on 40', [Wirtz](wiki:Florian_Wirtz) (assisted by [Barcola](wiki:Bradley_Barcola)) on 54' and [Isak](wiki:Alexander_Isak) (assisted by Wirtz) on 89' added two more, and [Nketiah](wiki:Eddie_Nketiah) pulled one back for Palace with a penalty on 90'+5. Palace had 19 shots (7 on target) and 53.1% possession; Liverpool had 20 shots (8 on target).

The core of the pre-match argument was "Liverpool's defeats came from rotation and the team's overall condition isn't bad; Palace have lost three straight with no goals and have big trouble in the attacking third". With Liverpool back to their core players, the overall attacking performance was stable, and coach Iraola swapping [Gakpo](wiki:Cody_Gakpo) and Barcola from one wing to the other at half-time was a masterstroke, bringing Barcola's pace and running into play and suppressing [Mitchell](wiki:Tyrick_Mitchell), who had sent over several threatening balls for Palace in the first half; it took a lot of the threat out of Palace's left side, and Barcola also used his pace on the counter to deliver a lovely cross from the wing for Wirtz to score. After going 3-0 up they conceded one, but it did no real harm.

On the pre-match injuries: Mateta wasn't in the squad (suspended, right); Timber and Strand Larsen both started (a late call, pre-match); Szoboszlai started and set up Mac Allister's header; and Gravenberch wasn't in the squad (Iraola's "only doubt"). Liverpool are on 18 points (fourth), Palace bottom on 7.

**Forest 3-1 City.** The City-win pick missed, but the "both teams to score" fallback landed ([Cherki](wiki:Rayan_Cherki) pulled one back on 52'), so it's a ⚠️. In attack they created a few decent chances but didn't take them, and the only goal was Cherki's 52' shot from the edge of the box, a spectacular effort after picking up a clearance that the opponent hadn't kicked far enough. The penalty they conceded can't be complained about, but given how they attacked this game, not winning was only what they deserved. ## Summary: Recent Form Matters, but It Isn't the Deciding Factor

This should be the lowest hit rate of any round so far, with only my two most confident picks, Liverpool and Arsenal, landing. Of the remaining four games, two were decided by low-probability events that were hard to foresee before the game: Chelsea's penalty and United's two remarkable stoppage-time goals. But I also got something wrong in both of those games: Chelsea's attack was worse than I expected, and United showed the resilience to fall behind twice and equalise twice (perhaps the atmosphere in their dressing room really is improving?). In the other two games, I underestimated how bad City's defence currently is and overestimated how stable Spurs' attack is. All in all, recent form is an important factor but not the deciding one: we can read the likely direction of a game from recent form, but it can still be affected by what happens on the day, and all I can say is that any bet carries risk, so please bet responsibly.
