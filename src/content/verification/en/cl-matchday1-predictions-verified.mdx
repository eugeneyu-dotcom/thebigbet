## A close scoreline isn't the same as a close match: the lesson from the Arsenal game

The most useful thing about Napoli vs Arsenal isn't that the pick was right — it's how it was right. The pre-match call was Arsenal to win comfortably away, but the final score was only 1-0, which on the surface reads like a nervy, narrow escape. Pull the underlying numbers and the picture is completely different — Arsenal had 26 shots to Napoli's 5, 7 shots on target to 3, 81 attacks to 21, and an xG of 4.05 to 0.22; Ødegaard was described post-match as having controlled the match for the full 90 minutes. This was a textbook case of total control paired with poor conversion, not a tense, tight contest that happened to end 1-0. That's the real methodology lesson here: judging whether a prediction was "accurate" shouldn't rely on the final scoreline alone — shots, shots on target, and xG are far more reliable evidence.

## Correct but no lock: the Liverpool match validated the "moderate confidence" call

The pre-match piece on Liverpool vs Atlético explicitly flagged "only moderate confidence," citing Barcola's debut integration and the ongoing right-back concern. The actual match validated that caution exactly — Atlético took the lead first through Llorente, and Liverpool needed a Szoboszlai equalizer and a Mac Allister winner to complete the comeback, in what was Iraola's Champions League managerial debut with the club. If the pre-match call had been "Liverpool win comfortably," this comeback would look like a shaky prediction. Because the confidence level and the defensive concern were both flagged up front, this match is actually the best demonstration of the reasoning framework working exactly as intended.

## United's win had no surprises — it was purely a gap in class

The pre-match piece on United vs Sabah leaned on the market's implied win probability of nearly 85% to make the case that this was a fixture with an enormous gap in class. United won 4-0, an even more comfortable margin than expected, with nothing further worth digging into — a direct confirmation of the read that when the class gap is large enough, poor recent form on one side doesn't change the conclusion.
