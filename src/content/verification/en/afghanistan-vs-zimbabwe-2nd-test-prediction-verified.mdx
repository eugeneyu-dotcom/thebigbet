## Zimbabwe by 57 Runs: The Draw Rested on a Match That Never Repeated

**How it went**: Zimbabwe won the toss and batted, reaching 250/5 on day 1 and 339 all out on day 2. Afghanistan were bowled out for 217 in 69.4 overs (from 154/6 they lost their last four for 63), a lead of 122. In the second innings Zimbabwe were 177/8 when [Muzarabani](wiki:Blessing_Muzarabani) and Cremer added 90 for the ninth wicket, and Zimbabwe declared on 295/9 to set 418. Afghanistan were 143/5 at the close of day 4 and 281/7 on day 5; Naseer Khan Maroofkhil (101\*) and Zazai (87) carried them to within 60 runs with 358/9, and the last wicket fell in the 131st over, 57 short.

**Wrong: "neither side can take 20 wickets."** This was the pillar of the draw pick, and 39 wickets fell in this match against 25 in the first Test. Zimbabwe took all 20 Afghan wickets (Muzarabani 6, Masekesa 6, Evans 4, Cremer 4); Afghanistan took 19 (Naseer Khan Maroofkhil 9, Ziaur Rahman 4, [Saleem](wiki:Mohammad_Saleem_(cricketer)) 3, [Yamin Ahmadzai](wiki:Yamin_Ahmadzai) 3). Runs per wicket fell from 61.6 in the first Test to 31.0 here (1,211 runs, 39 wickets), and Zimbabwe declared with one wicket in hand, so Afghanistan were a single wicket short of bowling them out twice as well.

**Wrong: "450 or more in an innings wouldn't be a surprise."** The highest innings was Afghanistan's 360 and none of the four reached 400. I assumed the second Test would be prepared like the first and flagged that I didn't know whether it would be the same pitch (⚠️). The figures say it did not play like the first: 39 wickets fell and the quickest innings, Afghanistan's 217, was over in 69.4 overs.

**Wrong: "nothing in it for the seamers."** The coaches' remark came from the first Test, and the pitch did not stay the same: for this Test the ground switched to a grassier strip (pitch six), which gave the bowlers more to work with. That also undoes the idea that the attacks lacked a "decisive weapon." Zimbabwe changed their attack as well, and Muzarabani (6 wickets in 53 overs) and Evans (4 in 45) used the bounce on offer, while Afghanistan found theirs in a debutant who was not among the names I had worked from.

**Right: Taylor and the batting edge.** I wrote that Zimbabwe had a slight batting edge, with [Taylor](wiki:Brendan_Taylor) as one of the two most experienced players in the series. Taylor made 119 and 50 and was Player of the Series (382 runs across the two Tests, counting the first Test's 131 and 82).

**Right: Yamin Ahmadzai and the weather.** I wrote that Yamin would come in if the pitch offered help, and he did (3 wickets, 41.5 overs). All five days had play, so the forecast of no rain held.

**Half right: head-to-head.** The article noted that Afghanistan had won the second Test of both earlier series after failing to win the first, but that both times they had [Rashid Khan](wiki:Rashid_Khan). He was absent again and the pattern did not repeat, so the record is now two wins each and two draws across six Tests.

## Summary: I Assumed the Pitch Would Not Change

My pre-match article took the first Test's pitch as given: on that surface the bowlers struggled whoever was bowling, and I carried the draw argument over to the second Test. But the ground switched to a grassier strip, Zimbabwe changed their attack, and Muzarabani and Evans used the bounce to take wickets. The pre-match article's closing note did say the second pitch might differ, but naming a risk is not the same as hedging against it: I did not build any deeper reasoning around a changed pitch, and that is the biggest reason the prediction missed.
