The 1st Test between New Zealand and India at the Basin Reserve in Wellington (19 to 23 November) is over. [My pre-match article](/en/cricket/predictions/india-vs-new-zealand-test-series-prediction/) picked **the draw**, and also suggested **a combined first-innings total of more than 700** and **a batter scoring a century in an innings**. **The match was drawn, so all three landed.** But my projection for India's first innings, 450 or more and a lead of 80 to 100, was far below the real number: India declared on 720/9, a lead of 352. India won the toss and chose to field. Player of the Match was Henry Nicholls (175\* and 31). Close of play: day 1, New Zealand 243/5; day 2, India 159/1 (39 overs), the score my pre-match article quoted; day 3, India 486/4; day 4, India declared on 720/9 in the afternoon and New Zealand were 119/2; day 5, New Zealand 239/5 when the match ended. Across the match only 24 wickets fell for 1,327 runs, 55.3 runs a wicket.

**Weather:** weather did not decide this match. The pre-match forecast showed little chance of rain over the five days, the only mention I found was a light drizzle with the covers on before the start of day 1 (play went on under bright skies after lunch), and neither the scorecard nor the match report records any play lost to rain. The draw came from the pitch and the timing of the declaration, not from the weather.

## The Draw Landed for the Reason I Gave, but I Never Wrote Down the Declaration

**Right: "the draw."** The main reason I gave, that with the first innings still running after two days neither side would have time to bowl the other out twice, held: only 24 wickets fell in the match, and New Zealand batted 120 overs in the second innings for the loss of 5.

**Right: "the pitch has become flatter, which is relatively unhelpful for bowlers."** New Zealand were 70/5 on day 1 (Bumrah 4-92), but then Nicholls and Phillips added 216, India made 720/9 and New Zealand scored at 1.99 an over for 120 overs in the second innings; match reports described the pitch as lifeless. The scorecard also shows the pitch was helping the bowlers on the morning of day 1 (New Zealand were 52/4), and my pre-match article only said it had "become flatter", without asking whether it had been like that on the first day.

**Wrong (the number was too cautious): "India should finish the first innings on 450 or more, a lead of 80 to 100."** India declared on 720/9, a lead of 352. I projected from the 159/1 at the close of day 2 at a rate of about 4.07 an over, and did not allow for India batting on into the afternoon of day 4.

**Right: "a first-innings total over 700" and "a batter century."** 1,088 runs and four centuries; India's top five (Jaiswal 85, Rahul 136, Padikkal 80, Gill 98, Pant 128) all made 80 or more.

**Right: "when the side batting first reaches 350, the side batting second has a good chance of passing 350, even 400."** New Zealand made 368 and India 720.

**Right: "India's batters look in excellent form."** India scored at 4.33 an over, with Jaiswal 85 off 89 balls, Jurel 78 off 79 and Pant 128 off 153.

**Missing before the match: when India would declare.** India declared on the afternoon of day 4 on 720/9 (166 overs), leaving about 120 overs to take 10 wickets, and took only 5. I only wrote that neither side would have time to bowl the other out twice, and did not discuss how the result could change if one side declared early. Some think India declared too late, but I think that, with this the first Test of the series, both sides' fairly cautious approach is understandable, and that was one of the reasons I picked the draw before the game.

**What the market said:** Bet365's model at the close of day 2 had the draw highest at 44.2%, ahead of India at 38.3% and New Zealand at 17.5%. The draw was the most likely result and it happened; India made 720 and still did not win.

On the second day I already thought a draw was the most likely result for this match: it was the first meeting of the series, so both sides might choose a cautious approach and not insist on forcing a result. The outcome did bear that out, and the two sides drew. But I was too cautious on the scores: I thought India would be fine with around 450 to 500, and never expected them to bat all the way past 700, declaring only on the afternoon of day 4. Some feel they declared too late, and that declaring earlier might have left time to take the remaining wickets. I think that by the time they were batting on day 4 the time was already gone, and a declaration in the morning or the afternoon would have made little difference. I also think this is what to watch for in the second Test: if either side wants to avoid a draw, how well they time the declaration will be the key to whether there is a result.
