## Heat 115-109 Jazz: The Winner Was Right, but the -9 Was Missed by 3 Points

**How it went**: quarter scores were Heat 29-35-28-23 and Jazz 25-29-18-37. Miami led 64-54 at half and 92-72 after three, by as many as 23; Utah won the fourth quarter 37-23, including an 11-0 run that cut it to 101-93, and it ended with a 6-point margin. The Heat led for 43:04 and the Jazz for 3:39, with just 1 lead change.

**Injuries and starters**: Miami started Larsson, Giannis, Adebayo, Thompson and Mitchell, with Wiggins coming off the bench; Hardaway Jr., whom I listed in the rotation beforehand, was a DNP (coach's decision). Utah were without George, Jackson and Nurkic, and **Markkanen, still questionable before the game, was confirmed out** (ESPN's injury page updated him to Out with a right wrist sprain around the time of the official report, and he doesn't appear in the official box score); that news was settled after my pre-match piece was published, so Utah started Green, Bailey, Hayes, Okogie and Peterson.

**Right: the Heat win.** Utah are 1-10 and have lost nine straight, missing four key players, and the Heat led for 43:04. My line "backing the Heat to win is a given" held up.

**Right: "Wiggins to the bench, Larsson into the starting five", and Giannis' wrist probable but playing.** [Larsson](wiki:Pelle_Larsson) started and had 14 points and 7 rebounds, while [Wiggins](wiki:Andrew_Wiggins) came off the bench and went 5/8 for 13 points in 18:32; [Giannis](wiki:Giannis_Antetokounmpo) played 30:34 (a little below his 31 and 34 in the last two games), the wrist had no obvious effect, and he finished with 30 points, 11 rebounds and 4 assists.

**Half right: "Reason one for the cover: Utah's frontcourt is thin, and Giannis or Adebayo can do what they want against it."** Giannis did go 12/21 for 30 points and 11 rebounds; but [Adebayo](wiki:Bam_Adebayo) was cold, shooting 1/8, and 9 of his 11 points came from the free-throw line (9/12), which isn't "do what they want".

**Half right: "Reason two for the cover: the Heat's pace is second in the league, Utah are short of a floor general, and they'll be pulled into a high-scoring tempo."** My formula estimate puts the game at about 104.5 possessions (above the Heat's season 102.6; this is an estimate, not the official Pace), so the tempo was there, but the total was only 224, under the 228.5 line, mainly because Miami's shooting was poor: 35/89 (39.3%) overall and 12/41 (29.3%) from three, under 40% and under 30%. On top of that, although Utah's 18 turnovers turned into 25 Heat points, fast-break points were Utah 17 to Heat 9, so Miami didn't turn the turnovers they forced into fast-break scoring.

**Right: "Thompson rolls a 1".** Before the game I said there was a 70% chance of covering, with the remaining 30% being [Thompson](wiki:Klay_Thompson) having an off night. Thompson went 3/13, 2/10 (20%) from three, and scored 11 points; and it wasn't just Thompson, since Adebayo also went 1/8 for 11 points, so two players rolled a 1 at once. That risk really did happen, and it's lucky the Jazz were so depleted that the Heat weren't upset.

**Wrong: "Utah haven't shot well from three over these 10 games (about 32%), so if the touch stays cold tonight, the Heat's biggest defensive flaw won't be exploited."** Utah went 13/33 (39.4%) from three, with [Collier](wiki:Isaiah_Collier) 4/4, [Peterson](wiki:Darryn_Peterson) 4/9 and [Bailey](wiki:Ace_Bailey_(basketball)) 3/5; Miami let Utah score 109 with a 55.9% effective field-goal percentage (eFG%), above Utah's season average of 106.2 points. The Heat's defensive problem is still there; it was just covered up by the free-throw and turnover gap.

**Right (for reference): what the market is saying.** The -9 missed, but the market's read on the winner (Heat about 74.9% with the margin removed) was correct; books were split between -9 and -9.5, with 19 at -9.5, a number further away and harder to cover than -9.

**The Heat won on free throws and turnovers.** Utah committed 30 fouls and 18 turnovers, and Miami shot 33/44 at the line; their field-goal and three-point percentages were far worse than Utah's (39.3% to 48.8%, 29.3% to 39.4%). Utah's Peterson (25 points) and Collier (17 points) both fouled out with 6.

## Summary: The Winner Was Right, but the Line Was Lost to Shooting Luck

The most interesting thing about this game is that the cover risks I listed beforehand, Thompson rolling a 1 and the margin getting cut down late, both happened. Thompson was 3/13 and Adebayo 1/8, so two of Miami's scorers went cold together, the team shot 39.3%, and Utah's 37-23 fourth quarter pulled a 20-point gap back to 6, so the Heat won by only 6 and missed the -9 by 3 points. The result of the game wasn't affected, but the spread didn't cover, which is bad luck: the winner was right, but the spread was lost because of players' shooting touch.

What I said before the game was a "70% chance of covering", and 70% is not 100%; this game was a demonstration of what the remaining 30% looks like. Miami's 33/44 at the line and Utah's 18 turnovers filled the hole left by the three-point shooting and saved the win.
