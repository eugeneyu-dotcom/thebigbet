**Injuries and starters**: the 76ers' starting five of LeBron, Embiid, Brown, Maxey and Edgecombe all played, each for 33 to 37 minutes, with nobody rested, so my biggest premise before the game, "full strength", held; the Pistons' absences, [Duren](wiki:Jalen_Duren) and [Isaiah Joe](wiki:Isaiah_Joe), matched what I had beforehand, and neither appears on the roster sheet.

Detroit only just held the margin together thanks to [Robinson](wiki:Duncan_Robinson)'s red-hot shooting from outside (7/11 from three) for 27 points.

**Right: the 76ers "score from many places."** Brown 30, Embiid 26, [Maxey](wiki:Tyrese_Maxey) 19, [LeBron](wiki:LeBron_James) 13 and [Edgecombe](wiki:VJ_Edgecombe) 11: all five played and all reached double figures.

## Summary: The Game Went Close to Expectations, and Detroit Again Showed No Second Ball Handler

The game went about as expected: without Duren, Detroit lacked the interior attacking and rim protection, with Reed shooting just 4 for 14 and fouling out. Detroit did score more in the paint, but that came from the 76ers choosing to trap Cunningham heavily after screens, forcing him to give the ball up and leaving cutters open, along with fast breaks. And the 76ers' plan of squeezing Cunningham did work well; although Robinson hit 7 of 11 from three for 27 points, several of those were heavily contested shots that had already been pressured into place, and it was simply unlucky that they went in, so overall the defensive plan still counts as a success. And Detroit exposed once more the problem we've mentioned many times, the lack of a second ball handler: in the key moments nobody could share the ball-handling load with Cunningham, who was forced into some difficult contested shots, and that is the root cause of his key three being blocked.
