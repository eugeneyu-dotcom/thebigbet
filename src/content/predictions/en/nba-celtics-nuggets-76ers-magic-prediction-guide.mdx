## 76ers @ Magic: Without Brown the 76ers Still Have More Weapons Than a Magic Team Without Banchero

**Tip-off**: 11/19 (Thu) 08:10 Taiwan time / 11/18 (Wed) 19:10 US Eastern. **Lines** (taken 11/18, Pinnacle): 76ers -2 (1.93) / Magic +2 (1.95); moneyline 76ers 1.76, Magic 2.16; total 222 (over 1.93 / under 1.94); with the margin removed, the 76ers are about 55.1% and the Magic about 44.9%.

**Recent form**: the 76ers are 12-3 and 4-1 over their last 5 with a +2.8 average margin, the 11/10 loss to the Celtics by 22 being the main drag; the Magic are 6-7 and have won 4 straight with a +6.6 average margin over their last 5, but 3 of those wins came against teams at 1-12, 2-13 and 1-13 (Kings, Jazz, Bucks), and only the 11/16 road win over the Celtics (11-3) is a genuinely good result. The quality has to be separated: all 3 of the 76ers' losses came against strong teams (Pistons, Cavaliers, Celtics) and all 8 of their wins against weak ones; the Magic are 2-4 against teams over .500.

**Injuries**: the 76ers are without Jaylen Brown (22.6 points and 34.7 minutes a game) and also Barlow and Bona, so their big-man rotation is down to Embiid, Wade, Walker and LeBron; the Magic are also missing Banchero, Isaac, Penda and Nelson, though the good news is that Suggs has just returned. The 76ers are a deep team this season and even without Brown they still have three scoring threats in Maxey, Embiid and LeBron, while the Magic depend mainly on their two forwards and, with Banchero out, are left with Franz Wagner (8.3 assists) to carry the game. **⚠️ This comes from the ESPN injury page compiled before the game; whether LeBron and Embiid play should be confirmed against the official injury report.**

**Rest and travel**: both teams have had one day off; the 76ers fly from Brooklyn to Orlando and just played a back-to-back on 11/15 and 11/16 (Embiid and LeBron each sat out one of the games), while the Magic flew home from Boston after beating the Celtics on 11/16, so the gap is small.

**Home/road**: the Magic are 1-4 at home (5-3 on the road) and the 76ers 6-1 on the road (6-2 at home), so home court has not clearly helped either team (the Magic's home sample is only 5 games, so don't treat it as settled).

**What the market says**: 76ers -2 (about 55% with the margin removed), so the market has already priced in Brown's absence and still makes the visitors the favorite; the total of 222 is close to the two teams' 223.2 average (76ers 225.3, Magic 221.0), and Brown's absence leaves the 76ers one scorer short.

**Depth**: the 76ers have about 9 usable players among starters and bench (Maxey, Embiid, LeBron, Edgecombe, Wade, KCP, Simons, Walker and so on), and without Brown their perimeter scoring comes from Maxey and Simons; the Magic also have about 9 (Wagner, Carter, Bane, da Silva, Black, Suggs, Richardson, Cain, Bitadze).

**Specific tactical weaknesses**: the Magic's weaknesses are turnovers (15.8 a game, the most of the four teams) and a three-point percentage (33.7%) near the bottom of the league, with an offensive rating of just 109.7, in the lower-middle of the league, and Banchero's injury has hurt their offense a lot; the 76ers' weakness is interior depth (Embiid shoots 40% and is 6 of 40 from three, and Barlow and Bona are both out), but the Magic's offense starts from the perimeter and has no one who can attack the paint directly.

**Matchups and key players**: Maxey (26.5 points) against Black and Suggs; Embiid (19.5 points, 8.5 rebounds) against Carter Jr. and Bitadze; whether LeBron plays (he has played only 11 of 15 games); and Franz Wagner (24.2 points, 8.3 assists) against the defense of Edgecombe and LeBron.

**Verdict**: **76ers moneyline (1.76) and 76ers -2 to cover (1.93), with a slight lean to the under (under 1.94).** On efficiency at both ends the 76ers are ahead across the board. Even without Brown, with so many creators Maxey, LeBron and company can still carry the offense. The Magic, by contrast, are hurt more by losing Banchero and can only rely on Wagner to break the defense down, but his game is built around attacking the rim rather than steady shooting, and the team's three-point percentage ranks near the bottom of the league, so there is little firepower behind him. Bane has been superb over the last four games, but with little support from the rest I still lean slightly to the 76ers to win on the strength of a defense that ranks near the top of the league this season and more scoring options.

**Total**: I lean slightly to the under. The total of 222 is close to the two teams' 223.2 average, and both teams are relatively strong on defense and weak on offense, so I lean to the under, but not strongly.

## Celtics @ Nuggets: A Moneyball Celtics Team Is Betting on Shooting Luck

**Tip-off**: 11/19 (Thu) 10:10 Taiwan time / 11/18 (Wed) 21:10 US Eastern. **Lines** (taken 11/18, Pinnacle): Celtics -2 (1.88) / Nuggets +2 (2.02); moneyline Celtics 1.73, Nuggets 2.21; total 222 (over 1.89 / under 1.98); with the margin removed, the Celtics are about 56.1% and the Nuggets about 43.9%.

**Recent form**: the Celtics are 11-3 and 4-1 over their last 5 with a +14.8 average margin, after losing at home to the Magic by 10 on 11/16; the Nuggets are 7-5 and 3-2 over their last 5 with a +0.6 average margin, after losing at home to the Suns by 12 on 11/16. The Celtics are 9-1 against weak teams and 2-2 against strong ones; the Nuggets are 6-1 against weak teams and only 1-4 against strong ones, so the gap between the teams comes mostly against strong opponents.

**Injuries**: ESPN lists no Celtics injuries (Tatum missed 11/4 and George missed 11/2 and 11/11, those three games being +7, -16 and +5); the Nuggets are missing only Strawther, and Cameron Johnson missed three games this season but has recently returned. **⚠️ This comes from the ESPN injury page compiled before the game; please go by the official injury report.**

**Rest and travel**: both teams have had one day off and the Celtics must fly to Denver (high altitude), but the Nuggets are only 2-3 at home, so the altitude edge hasn't shown up for them (a 5-game home sample, so don't treat it as settled).

**Home/road**: the Nuggets are 2-3 at home and 5-2 on the road; the Celtics are 5-2 on the road (losses to the Pistons and Rockets) and 6-1 at home (the loss to the Magic).

**What the market says**: Celtics -2 (about 56% with the margin removed), a road favorite in Denver, so the market thinks the Celtics are slightly stronger even without home court; the total of 222 is almost exactly the two teams' 222.5 average (Celtics 218.1, Nuggets 227.0).

**Depth**: the Celtics have about 10 usable players among starters and bench (Jayson Tatum, Paul George, White, Pritchard, Hauser, Queta, Scheierman, Walsh, Robinson, Garza); the Nuggets lean on their starters (Jokić, Murray, Braun, Gordon and C. Johnson all play 29 to 36 minutes), and their bench is just DeRozan, Brazile, Bagley and Jones.

**Specific tactical weaknesses**: the Celtics have played a moneyball style for several seasons now, relying on a high volume of threes (43.9 a game at 38.2%), and although that approach did win them a championship and is very strong on expected value, a style without a steadier way to score can lose to the worst teams in the league when everyone's shot is off. The Nuggets are the opposite: they take the fewest threes (30.9) and grab the fewest rebounds (41.7), but keep their offense efficient through Jokić's high-efficiency passing system, with a team assist rate (67.4%) and low turnover rate (12.1%) that are both the best in the league. But the reason the Nuggets' record isn't as strong as in previous years is a decline on defense, allowing 113.2 points a game.

**Matchups and key players**: Jokić (24.1 points, 10.9 rebounds, 8.9 assists) against Queta, Robinson and Garza; Murray (26.1 points) against White and Pritchard; Jayson Tatum (26.5 points) against Gordon and Braun; whether Paul George plays (he has played 12 of 14 games); and DeRozan off the bench with 9.2 points but 2 of 14 from three. Whether the Celtics' threes fall will decide whether this is a blowout.

**Verdict**: **Celtics moneyline (1.73) and Celtics -2 to cover (1.88), with a slight lean to the over (over 1.89).** This season the Celtics rank in the league's top five on both offense and defense, and George has bounced back after changing teams, finding his scoring touch again and showing none of the old slow-footed veteran on defense. On the Nuggets' side, Aaron Gordon has been in poor form this season and can't even deliver the defense he is known for, and with Jokić and Murray not being strong defenders themselves, Denver's offense stays efficient while its defense is poor. And Jokić, long known for his consistency, still keeps the Nuggets' passing system running this season but looks increasingly strained on the court, and his repeated failures to get calls near the rim are a major warning sign. **But as I said above, the weakness of the Celtics' moneyball style is that when nobody's shot is falling, they can lose to anyone.** My [first prediction on the Celtics this season](/en/nba/predictions/nba-knicks-celtics-pistons-heat-rockets-spurs-prediction-guide/) missed because Tatum's three-point shooting was poor in that game (see the [verification](/en/nba/verification/nba-knicks-celtics-pistons-heat-rockets-spurs-predictions-verified/)), and that is the biggest risk in backing Boston.

**Total**: I lean slightly to the over. The Celtics have the slowest pace (estimated about 98.5 possessions) and average 218.1 total points, the Nuggets 227.0, with the line of 222 between the two, but given that both teams are in the league's top five on offense, and the Nuggets' poor defense this season, I lean slightly to the over. (Possession counts are my own estimates from box scores, not official numbers.)

## Losing a Star Isn't Simple Arithmetic, and Two Paths Have No Standard Answer

These two games are interesting. One is a contest between two playoff-caliber Eastern teams that both have key players hurt, which makes the call harder. My view is that losing Banchero hurts the Magic more than losing Brown hurts the 76ers, so I favor the 76ers, but losing a star is sometimes not simple addition and subtraction and comes down to a whole team's chemistry. The other is a contest between two former champions, both of which came under salary pressure after winning titles and made many changes or compromises to extend their title windows, yet chose very different paths: Denver kept most of its core and made small adjustments to hold on, while Boston tore things down and decisively broke up its two-star pairing. This game can also be seen as a contest between those two paths. Still, the Celtics' moneyball style means that predicting their games always carries risk, so please bet responsibly.
