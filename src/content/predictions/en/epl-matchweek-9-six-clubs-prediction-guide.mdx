## Chelsea vs Man United: Both Sides Have Flaws, but Chelsea Are the Ones Playing an Extra Midweek Game

**Kick-off**: 10/31 (Sat) 20:30 Taiwan time / 12:30 local, Stamford Bridge. **Odds** (fetched 10/28, average of 43 bookmakers): Chelsea 1.79 / draw 3.94 / United 4.08 (de-vigged 53% / 24% / 23%); over 2.5 goals 1.54 / under 2.5 goals 2.42 (only 9 quotes, for reference only).

**Recent form**: Chelsea are 4W 1D 3L (13 points, 5th), and lost 0-2 at home to Spurs last time out (only 7 shots, [Palmer](wiki:Cole_Palmer) sent off); United are winless in eight across all competitions, sit 16th after the 0-3 loss to Bournemouth, and are just 1 point off the relegation zone.

**Injuries and squad**: Chelsea are without [Reece James](wiki:Reece_James) (minor knee surgery) and [João Pedro](wiki:João_Pedro_(footballer,_born_2001)) (thigh, out for weeks), [Emiliano Martínez](wiki:Emiliano_Martínez) (after jaw surgery) is doubtful, and [Mike Penders](wiki:Mike_Penders) has started the last two; Palmer is back from suspension. For United, [Mainoo](wiki:Kobbie_Mainoo), [Rashford](wiki:Marcus_Rashford) and [Shaw](wiki:Luke_Shaw) are all doubts, and [Bruno Fernandes](wiki:Bruno_Fernandes) was moved to the bench last time, so whether he starts here is one to watch.

**Fixture congestion (EFL Cup)**: Chelsea travel to Liverpool for the EFL Cup fourth round on Wednesday (10/28, Anfield) before hosting United on Saturday, with no European football; United were knocked out of the EFL Cup (lost 2-3 to Brighton in round three) and have the whole week to prepare, though after this game they host Roma in the Champions League on Tuesday (11/3) with only three days' rest. **This midweek difference is the easiest variable to overlook in this game**: Chelsea lack a full rest, while United have plenty.

**Home/away form**: Chelsea at home are 2W 1D 1L (11 scored, 8 conceded); **United away are 0W 3D 1L (4 scored, 6 conceded)**, still without an away win this season.

**Market attitude**: The market makes Chelsea 53% favourites with the draw at 24%; United's 23% is one of the lowest away-win probabilities this round.

**Verdict**: Neither side's form is much to look at. Chelsea's problem is injuries: first Reece James, and now João Pedro up front too, leaving [Morgan Rogers](wiki:Morgan_Rogers) to carry the line; the good news is Palmer should be back from suspension, and if they can fix their build-up from the back they should still pose enough of a threat going forward. United, as everyone knows, are caught in a dressing-room storm and look completely lost on the pitch; Fernandes, moved to the bench, hasn't solved the attacking problem and has drained the whole team's creativity, defending has been a recurring issue, and I doubt that gets better before a change of manager. So in a game where both sides are flawed, I'm still **picking Chelsea to win**, but given both sides' form, **my fallback is the under**.

## Man City vs Brighton: Brighton's High-Tempo Football Meets a City Midfield That Doesn't Fear It

**Kick-off**: 10/31 (Sat) 23:00 Taiwan time / 15:00 local, Etihad. **Odds** (fetched 10/28, average of 43 bookmakers): City 1.63 / draw 4.37 / Brighton 4.63 (de-vigged 58% / 22% / 20%); main goals line **3.5** (over 2.13 / under 1.70, 16 quotes).

**Recent form**: After their first loss to Villa, City meet Brighton first in the EFL Cup on 10/28; Brighton have scored 23 and conceded 7 in 8 games, the league's best attack, lost 0-2 to Liverpool last time out, and before that beat Palace 5-0. Brighton's **home record is 3W 1D (13 scored, 1 conceded) and away is 2W 2L (10 scored, 6 conceded)**, and this one is away.

**Injuries and squad**: City are without [Semenyo](wiki:Antoine_Semenyo) but their other key players are back; for Brighton, [Kadıoğlu](wiki:Ferdi_Kadıoğlu) and [Chema Andrés](wiki:Chema_Andrés) picked up hip injuries against Liverpool and their status is unclear, [Mitoma](wiki:Kaoru_Mitoma) is out, and sources differ on [Wieffer](wiki:Mats_Wieffer).

**Fixture congestion**: City face Brighton twice in a row (cup then league), plus a Champions League trip to Leipzig on 11/4, so Maresca's rotation is the key.

**Home/away form**: City are 4 from 4 at home (11 scored, 6 conceded) and 3W 1L away (including the Villa defeat).

**Tactical style**: Brighton have produced very good attacking intensity this season with a high-tempo, pressing style, and have beaten Arsenal, currently level on points at the top, 3-0; with City also conceding often at the back lately, this won't be an easy game for them.

**Market attitude**: The 3.5 main goals line is the highest of the round, so the market expects an open game; City's 58% win probability is the second highest of the four games.

**Verdict**: Although City lost to Villa last round, I expect them to rotate in the EFL Cup and then face Brighton in the league with their full strength. Compared with Arsenal and others, I think City are relatively less afraid of Brighton's high-tempo style: City's midfield is physically strong, harder and more aggressive in pressing and running, and better on the ball and in attack, so Brighton's pace is more likely to expose the space behind them. So I think this one will be a **City win**, but given how often City have been conceding, **my fallback is both teams to score**, and even over 3.5 isn't a stretch for these two.

So against a Palace side playing very badly on the road, I think **Spurs winning is not a problem**. ## Liverpool vs Arsenal: A Real Heavyweight Game, and I'm Going Against the Market with Liverpool

**Kick-off**: 11/2 (Mon) 00:30 Taiwan time / 11/1 (Sun) 16:30 local, Anfield. **Odds** (fetched 10/28, average of 43 bookmakers): Liverpool 2.98 / draw 3.46 / Arsenal 2.30 (de-vigged 32% / 27% / 41%); over 2.5 goals 1.84 / under 2.5 goals 1.95 (20 quotes). **Away side Arsenal are favourites**.

**Recent form**: Arsenal have 21 points (level with City at the top) and won 2-0 against Everton last time out ([Konsa](wiki:Ezri_Konsa) and [Gabriel](wiki:Gabriel_Magalhães) each scored), having lost only to Brighton in their last five; Liverpool have 15 points (4th) and won 2-0 against Brighton last time out ([Barcola](wiki:Bradley_Barcola) assisting [Isak](wiki:Alexander_Isak)), unbeaten in four across all competitions since losing 0-3 to City, so their form is not to be underestimated either.

**Injuries and squad**: For Arsenal, [Saka](wiki:Bukayo_Saka) and [Saliba](wiki:William_Saliba) are out and [Calafiori](wiki:Riccardo_Calafiori) is a doubt; for Liverpool, [Szoboszlai](wiki:Dominik_Szoboszlai), [Ekitike](wiki:Hugo_Ekitike), [Frimpong](wiki:Jeremie_Frimpong) and [Bradley](wiki:Conor_Bradley) are out, and [Wirtz](wiki:Florian_Wirtz) and [Gakpo](wiki:Cody_Gakpo) are doubts. **Both sides are missing key players**, which is the biggest variable in this game.

**Fixture congestion (EFL Cup)**: Liverpool host Chelsea in the EFL Cup fourth round on Wednesday (10/28) and Arsenal travel to Fleetwood Town midweek; after this game both have Champions League away trips on Wednesday (Liverpool to Fenerbahçe in Turkey, Arsenal to Slavia Praha in the Czech Republic) with only three days' rest. **From last Sunday's game against Brighton to the Champions League on 11/4, Liverpool play four games in 11 days** (Brighton → EFL Cup Chelsea → Arsenal → Champions League Fenerbahçe), so rotation and fatigue are the biggest things to watch.

**Home/away form**: **Liverpool at home are only 1W 2D 1L, with just 4 goals in 4 games (5 conceded); away they are 3W 1D (7 scored, 3 conceded)**; Arsenal are 4 from 4 at home and 3W 1L away (4 scored, 3 conceded). **Anfield has not been a strength for Liverpool this season**, and that is reflected in the odds, with Liverpool at only 32%.

**Market attitude**: The market puts the away side Arsenal at 41% (Liverpool at home are just 32%), with the de-vigged draw at 27%, the highest draw probability of the four games.

**Verdict**: This is a real heavyweight game, fourth against second. Liverpool's recovery in form is clear; since joining Iraola's system, Barcola has shown a very good fit, and although he hasn't opened his Premier League account yet, he has set up several goals and has good chemistry with Isak; his ability to dribble past defenders down the flank always tears the opposition's line open, and with his active defending and pressing he is an excellent counter-press trigger in the system. As for Arsenal, even without Saka and Saliba they held Bayern and then won 2-0 against Everton, so their attack seems not to have been affected much, and together with their already excellent defence they have drawn level with City at the top. But I think their attacking problems are only temporarily masked rather than solved, and the weakness up front is still there. So in this one I'm going to be a little bold and go against the market: **Liverpool will win on the strength of their attack**. As for a fallback, the odds on both goals lines are poor, so I won't give it much thought; if I had to pick one, I'd take over 2.5.

None of this will be known until the official team news and the EFL Cup results are out, and whether United's dressing-room storm is affecting players' commitment on the pitch isn't something any model can quantify.
