Today's game is **the Heat on the road against the East-leading 76ers**. Miami started the season 0-4 and were 3-6 after nine games, then reeled off seven straight wins. That run does include the Pistons, Rockets and Thunder, teams with short title odds before the season, but all three are dealing with serious injury problems right now. Still, the last time I wrote about the Heat, in [Cavaliers vs Heat](/en/nba/predictions/nba-cavaliers-heat-prediction-guide/), I went against the market on Cleveland and Miami taught me a lesson ([verification](/en/nba/verification/nba-cavaliers-heat-predictions-verified/)), so I'm not going to underestimate them again. The 76ers are 14-4 and top of the East, but without Embiid last time out they lost at home to Portland by 17, and with Embiid ill he is likely to miss this one too. The line is only **76ers -2**: the East's best team laying just 2 points at home tells you the market no longer dares to take this streaking Heat side lightly. Miami have a question of their own, though: Giannis missed the last game with a groin problem and is still questionable. I'll work through it by recent form, injuries, rest and home/road, what the market is saying, depth and specific tactical weaknesses.

## Heat @ 76ers: Can Philadelphia's Frontcourt Without Embiid Hold Up Against Miami's Two Bigs?

**Tip-off**: 11/26 (Thu) 06:10 Taiwan time / 11/25 (Wed) 17:10 US Eastern, in Philadelphia (two hours earlier than the usual evening games). **Lines** (taken 11/25 around 23:30 Taiwan time, Pinnacle): 76ers -2 (1.98) / Heat +2 (1.90); moneyline Heat 2.07, 76ers 1.83; total 238 (over 2.02 / under 1.85); with the margin removed, about 53.1% 76ers and 46.9% Heat. This is the two teams' first meeting of the season. (Records, averages and shooting numbers below come from ESPN box scores and are my own totals, not official figures.)

**Recent form**: the Heat have won **seven straight**, scoring 126.7 and allowing 115.4 a game during the run, a +11.3 margin, including wins over the Pistons by 20, the Rockets by 18 and the Thunder by 10, **but all three of those teams are dealing with injuries right now, so those were depleted rosters**. The 76ers are 4-1 in their last five, but the wins came against the Pacers, Nets, Magic and Bucks (only by 3 against the 2-16 Bucks), and **the one good team they faced, Portland, beat them by 17**. Philadelphia are 3-3 against teams over .500, and all four of their losses have been by 13 or more; Miami are 4-5 against teams over .500, but have won their last three such games (Pistons, Rockets, Thunder), all by 10 or more.

**Injuries**: **the 76ers are likely without Embiid** (20.4 points and 8.9 rebounds this season, listed by ESPN as doubtful with an illness). They are 2-2 without him this season and lost both games against strong teams (Pistons, Trail Blazers). **For Miami, the key is Giannis** (28.2 points and 12.6 rebounds on 60% shooting, questionable with right groin tendinitis; ESPN says he got through most of Tuesday's practice). Without him the Heat still beat the Thunder by 10 (Adebayo 24, Larsson 21), but that was at home, against a Thunder team whose injury situation this season is even worse. Maxey and Brown both returned for Philadelphia in the last game. **⚠️ This is the pre-game list (from ESPN); check the official injury report for whether either star plays.**

**Rest and travel**: the Heat have had two days off since beating the Thunder on 11/22; the 76ers have had one since losing to Portland on 11/23. Philadelphia's next two games are a back-to-back (at Indiana on 11/27, at Houston on 11/28). This game is not one, but LeBron and Embiid have often sat one game of a back-to-back this season, so a rest day is still possible. Miami fly from Miami to Philadelphia, and **the last five games of their streak were all at home, so this is the first time during the run they face a strong team on the road**.

**Home/road**: the 76ers are 7-3 at home and actually 7-1 on the road, and all three home losses (Pistons, Celtics, Trail Blazers) came against teams over .500; the Heat are 6-3 at home and 4-3 on the road, and apart from Atlanta their road wins came against weak teams (Clippers, Kings, Jazz). **Philadelphia's home court hasn't been anything special this season.**

**What the market is saying**: 76ers -2 is about 53.1% with the margin removed, and since ESPN listed Embiid as doubtful back on 11/24, **this line should already account for his absence**; Giannis is still undecided, and if he is confirmed in, the line may move toward Miami. **The total is 238, with the under priced at 1.85 against 2.02 for the over, so the market leans slightly under.** The Heat average a combined 240.1 for the season and 249.8 in their last five; the 76ers 228.7 for the season and 250.0 in their last five. Both teams' recent games have landed around 250, yet the market opened at 238 and leans under, probably because it has priced in Embiid's absence and the uncertainty over Giannis.

**Depth**: without Embiid, the 76ers have about nine or ten players available (Maxey, Brown, LeBron, Edgecombe, Simons, Caldwell-Pope, Walker, Wade, Bona, Barlow), **and the only bigs left are Jabari Walker, Bona, Barlow, Wade and LeBron as an occasional fill-in**; the Heat run about nine (Giannis, Adebayo, Davion Mitchell, Klay Thompson, Larsson, Wiggins, Portis, Conwell, Gardner), and Hardaway Jr. has been a DNP for five straight games, so the rotation has tightened.

**Specific tactical weaknesses**:

- **Philadelphia's paint without Embiid**: against Portland on 11/23, Clingan pulled down 18 rebounds, five of them offensive, and Clingan and Robert Williams III alone had nine offensive rebounds, as many as the whole 76ers team. At the rim it was worse: Clingan went 7 of 8, and Portland shot over 50% as a team (46 of 90). The Heat's free-throw attempts over their last five games were 37, 40, 25, 44 and 21, and Giannis and Adebayo will keep attacking the paint; whether Walker, Bona and Barlow can hold up, and stay out of foul trouble, is the most direct matchup question in this game.
- **Miami's three-point defense**: the [season check](/en/nba/trends/nba-miami-heat-2026-27-season-check/) called it the Heat's biggest problem. Opponents shot 39.4% from three in the first 11 games and 35.8% in the last five, a slight improvement, but the sample is too small to draw conclusions. Then again, the 76ers aren't a three-point team to begin with (34.1 attempts a game at 34.9%, and only 9 of 33 on 11/23); their outside shooters are Simons (40.6%), Maxey and Caldwell-Pope.
- **Miami's turnovers**: the Heat average 14.0 turnovers a game to Philadelphia's 11.4; against the Bucks on 11/18 they turned it over 20 times and were outscored 25-44 in the fourth quarter, so Miami can switch off with a lead. Maxey and Edgecombe are quick, and the transition game is how Philadelphia punish turnovers.
- **Philadelphia's defense has slipped lately**: they have allowed 124.0 points a game over their last five (about 109.3 in the first 13), but key players have been missing in rotation during that stretch, so it's hard to say yet whether it's an unsettled rotation or a genuine decline.

**Key matchups**: Giannis (if he plays) against Walker, Barlow or LeBron; Adebayo against Walker and Bona; Maxey (25.7 points) against Davion Mitchell; Brown (22.4 points) against Wiggins and Larsson; LeBron's half-court playmaking (6.5 assists) against Miami's switching; and Klay Thompson's three-point shooting (43.2%).

**Verdict**: **Heat +2 (the 2.07 moneyline works too), on the condition that Giannis plays; if Giannis sits, I switch to 76ers -2.** If Giannis is able to play normally, I'm on the Heat here. The 76ers may be top of the East, but they are only 3-3 against teams over .500 this season, and they are likely without Embiid, which leaves a frontcourt of rotation bigs like Walker, Bona and Barlow, exactly where Miami are strongest. Giannis and Adebayo have been getting to the line a lot lately: against the Pistons, the two attacked a paint missing Duren for a combined 54 points and 22 free-throw attempts; against a relatively weak Bucks frontcourt they combined for 53 points and 29 attempts, and a 76ers frontcourt without Embiid may not be much better than either of those two.

The loss to Portland also shows some of the problems the 76ers have run into as the season goes on. First, the defensive intensity has dropped: LeBron's defense early in the season was impressive, but he is over 40, and as the season wears on he is struggling to keep up, while without Embiid the whole team's rim protection falls off sharply. Second, the offense has drifted into isolation; these stars all have the individual talent, but leaning too hard on one-on-one play isn't a good sign. Finally, the three-pointer: as the season check said, Miami have some problems defending the three, but Philadelphia aren't really set up to punish that right now.

**But this pick carries two big risks**: first, if Giannis sits, it's hard to say whether Miami can repeat the Thunder performance on the road; second, the last five games of the streak were all at home, and this is the first time in a while they go on the road to face a strong team.

**On the total**: both teams' last five games have totalled around 250, and Miami are the faster of the two (my box-score estimate of possessions is about 104.8 for the Heat and 100.9 for the 76ers), so at 238 I lean slightly to the **over**; but Embiid's absence takes away one of Philadelphia's scoring sources and the market leans a little to the under, so it isn't a strong lean.

## Two Big Offseason Trades, Two Completely Opposite Starts

This is a meeting of two teams at the top of the East that both made big moves before the season: [LeBron joining Philadelphia](/en/nba/trends/76ers-lbj-2026-27-outlook/) and Giannis joining Miami were probably the two biggest stories of the offseason. And the two teams' starts could hardly have been more different. Miami opened badly: the two bigs couldn't find a way to coexist and the defense had problems, to the point that I wrote a whole piece examining what was wrong, and only after the rotation changed and this seven-game run did they start looking like a strong team again. Philadelphia, by contrast, came together surprisingly quickly and charged to the top of the East, but with injuries and fatigue piling up they have started to look a little worn lately. Overall I lean slightly toward Miami, but this is a 76ers home game, Miami have players like Adebayo and Klay Thompson who can go 1-for-something on any given night this season, and my picks have missed plenty of times this season, so my confidence isn't high.
