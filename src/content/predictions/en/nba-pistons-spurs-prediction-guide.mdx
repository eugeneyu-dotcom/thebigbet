## Pistons @ Spurs: Winning Is Easy for San Antonio, Winning by 10 Is the Question

**Tip-off**: 11/18 (Wed) 09:10 Taiwan time / 11/17 (Tue) 20:10 US Eastern. **Lines** (taken 11/17, Pinnacle): Spurs -9 (1.90) / Pistons +9 (1.98); moneyline Pistons 4.20, Spurs 1.25; total 223 (over 1.94 / under 1.93); with the margin removed, the Spurs are about 77.1% and the Pistons about 22.9%.

**Recent form**: the Spurs have won 12 straight and have a +19.8 average margin over their last 5 (+22, +26, +5, +38, +8); the Pistons are 2-3 over their last 5 with a -3.2 average margin (-6, -5, +11, +4, -20). Neither record is very high in quality, though: 6 of the Spurs' 12 wins came against teams currently 4-9 or worse (Kings ×2, Jazz ×3, Bulls), and the margins weren't always big (Jazz +6 and +8, Bulls +5); of the Pistons' 8 wins, they are 5-0 against teams under .500 but only 3-4 against teams over .500. **Even so, the Spurs' league-best net rating is the real thing.**

**Injuries**: the Pistons recently lost Robinson (he left the 11/14 game against the Heat with a left quadriceps strain, and ESPN lists him as Out), and Duren and [Isaiah Joe](wiki:Isaiah_Joe) went down not long after the season began, so they badly lack shooting and size. Holland II and Prince are also hurt, and Huerter and Jenkins are expected to fill in. The Spurs have been without [Harris](wiki:Tobias_Harris) and Quaintance since the start of the season (neither has played), and were without [Fox](wiki:De'Aaron_Fox) in the last game, but according to reports he may return tonight. **The key lineup question: with Robinson out, Huerter is the only shooter the Pistons can really trust from outside, and whether they can threaten the Spurs from the perimeter is a big deal. ⚠️ This comes from the ESPN injury page compiled before the game, so please go by the official injury report.**

**Rest and travel**: the Pistons have had two days off and the Spurs one, a slight edge for Detroit, but neither is on a back-to-back, so the gap is small.

**Home/road**: the Spurs are 5-2 at home (the two losses were the first two games, to the Thunder and the Rockets) and 7-0 on the road, so home court shows no obvious gap for them; the Pistons are 5-2 on the road (losses to the Knicks and Heat) and 3-2 at home, so they aren't especially bad away. Home court is worth maybe 2 to 3 points here and isn't the main variable.

**What the market says**: Spurs -9/-9.5 (about 77% with the margin removed), with 15 of 32 books at -9.5 and 8 at -9, so the market sees this as a lopsided game; the total is 223 (most books at 221.5), and the two teams average about 225, so the total sits slightly under their averages and the market expects Detroit's scoring to drop without its shooters. The spread is roughly "the Spurs have won by 12.9 on average over their last 14, minus a discount for home court, opposition strength and the Pistons' injuries," **which means the market has already priced in Robinson's absence**.

**Depth**: the Spurs rely mainly on their starting five ([Wembanyama](wiki:Victor_Wembanyama), Fox, [Castle](wiki:Stephon_Castle), [Harper](wiki:Dylan_Harper) and [Vassell](wiki:Devin_Vassell) all average 28 to 31 minutes), and their bench of Champagnie, [Keldon Johnson](wiki:Keldon_Johnson), Barnes and Kornet has nobody averaging more than 10 points; the Pistons have only about nine usable players in total (Cunningham, Ausar Thompson, [Collins](wiki:John_Collins_(basketball)), [Reed](wiki:Paul_Reed_(basketball)), Jenkins, Huerter, Green, Smith and so on), clearly less depth than San Antonio. Without Robinson, Duren and Joe, their big-man rotation is down to Collins, Reed, Smith and Jones.

**Specific tactical weaknesses**: Detroit's weakness is a paint without Duren, with rebounds at 44.4 to 45.8 for opponents, while the Spurs are at 48.6 to 42.6 in rebounds and 54.7 to 43.7 in points in the paint, and Wembanyama averages 4.3 blocks; the best example of Detroit's paint being overrun is the 10/27 loss to the Knicks ([Towns](wiki:Karl-Anthony_Towns) had 20 rebounds, with Duren already hurt). San Antonio's weakness is perimeter defense (opponents shoot 38.2% from three) and the occasional absence of a starter; for Detroit to get within 9, they would probably need to hit threes, but Robinson isn't there. (Efficiency and possession figures are my own estimates from box scores, not official numbers.)

**Matchups and key players**: Wembanyama (28.6 points, 4.3 blocks) against Collins and Reed; Cunningham (25.7 points, 7.9 assists) against the Castle, Harper and Fox backcourt rotation; whether [Ausar Thompson](wiki:Ausar_Thompson) (8.6 rebounds, 5.1 assists) can make up for Duren's rebounding; and Huerter filling Robinson's slot (38 minutes on 11/14, 24 points on 11/8).

**Verdict**: **Spurs moneyline (1.25), and I like the Spurs -9 to cover (1.90), with a slight lean to the under (under 1.93).** On the winner, the Spurs extending the streak to 13 is close to a given, and the -9 line shows the market sees a one-sided game. Without Duren, the Pistons have nobody to stop Wembanyama in the paint, and without Robinson they've lost an important source of firepower, so opening things up from outside will be hard. We did [point out the weaknesses of the Spurs' three-guard lineup](/en/nba/predictions/nba-knicks-celtics-pistons-heat-rockets-spurs-prediction-guide/) early in the season, but right now the Pistons have no big or highly mobile wing who can punish that lineup, and in a contest of rotation speed the three guards have the edge. All in all, Cunningham's numbers this season are astonishing, but it would be very hard for him alone to hold off three guards plus Wembanyama inside. The only question is whether the Spurs can win by 10 or more (at Pinnacle's -9 a 9-point win is a push, while the -9.5 at most books needs 10), and given the Pistons' injuries I think the Spurs have about a 70% chance of winning by 10; the other 30% would be a fluke or garbage time arriving too early, since the Spurs' starters have played a lot of minutes this season and resting them early is actually good for the long season.

**Total**: I lean slightly to the under. The total of 223 is close to the two teams' averages, but without Robinson Detroit's offense drops while its defense may actually improve, and I expect San Antonio may give its bench more minutes, so I lean to the under.

## One Man Can Carry a Game, Not a Team

Detroit's injuries are heavy tonight and they can only lean on Cunningham, who has been remarkable lately: 52 points in 48 minutes on 11/12, a one-man demolition of the Wizards, and 33 more on 11/14, though this time it didn't stop a 20-point loss to the Heat. I strongly back him for First Team and even MVP this year, but modern basketball isn't a one-man game and he needs more help. Across from him is another First Team and MVP candidate in Wembanyama, who after a small early-season slump is now at an astonishing 28.6 points, 11.6 rebounds and 4.3 blocks, and even with Duren healthy it might not be enough against him, let alone with Duren out.
