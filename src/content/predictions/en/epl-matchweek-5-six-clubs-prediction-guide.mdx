Matchweek 4 of the Premier League is done, and matchweek 5 is up next. This breaks down the six focus clubs' fixtures the same way as always, through **current form, injuries and squad depth, and home/away advantage**, so we can read the matches together. If you ask me, the most interesting one this round has to be Spurs vs Villa — two sides who haven't won a match yet, so how does this one even end?

## Brentford vs Chelsea: the Blues control the ball, but the leaky defense is an old problem

**Current form**: Chelsea sit 7th on 7 points (see the [official Premier League table](https://www.premierleague.com/tables)), coming off a 2-2 home draw with Hull City — [João Pedro](wiki:João_Pedro_(footballer,_born_2001)) equalized in the 66th minute, but they actually controlled possession for most of the match and still couldn't avoid the draw. Brentford are 7th on 6 points, though their recent form is of limited use as a signal — their last official match was a League Cup win over Reading, not a league fixture.

**Injuries**: Brentford are shorthanded across the board — [Nathan Collins](wiki:Nathan_Collins), Josh Dasilva, Antoni Milambo, Sepp van den Berg, and Kafe Furo are all out. Chelsea only have Marco Palestra confirmed absent, with [Moises Caicedo](wiki:Moisés_Caicedo), Emmanuel Emegha, and Jordan Henderson all doubts — but [João Pedro](wiki:João_Pedro_(footballer,_born_2001)), Morgan Rogers, and [Cole Palmer](wiki:Cole_Palmer) are all expected to lead the frontline regardless. As long as that front-three trident is intact, Chelsea still have a puncher's chance no matter who else is out. And the back line? Please — since when did Chelsea need one of those this season?

**Structural weakness**: Chelsea have basically been slugging it out all season — the defense is essentially just "how many can Big Martin save." So the result comes down almost entirely to attacking firepower: conceding under two is a success, and not scoring three is a failure.

**Head-to-head**: Chelsea won the last meeting 2-0.

**Verdict**: Brentford's injury list is far worse than Chelsea's, and there's a real gap in quality too, so a Chelsea win shouldn't be an issue. But given how leaky that Chelsea defense still is — same as last round — I'd rather back Both Teams to Score or the Over than the straight win market.

## Spurs vs Aston Villa: this round's weirdest fixture, neither side has won a match yet

**Current form**: this match's current-form picture doesn't match any other fixture this round — Spurs have only 2 points from 4 games, 17th in the table, and Villa are even worse off with 1 point from 4, 19th. Even more absurd: Spurs haven't scored a single goal in 4 league matches this season, and Villa haven't scored in their last 3 either. Both sides stuck in a serious goal drought is a harder situation to read than one team's slump alone, because there's no obvious answer to "which side is the less broken one."

**Injuries**: Spurs' absentee list is scary long — [Sandro Tonali](wiki:Sandro_Tonali), Xavi Simons, Wilson Odobert, [Dejan Kulusevski](wiki:Dejan_Kulusevski), [Pedro Porro](wiki:Pedro_Porro), and [Mykhailo Mudryk](wiki:Mykhailo_Mudryk) are all out injured, plus the suspended Richarlison — basically every key position from midfield to the flanks is on the treatment table. Villa aren't much better off, with [Pau Torres](wiki:Pau_Torres), [Amadou Onana](wiki:Amadou_Onana), Leon Goretzka, and [Ian Maatsen](wiki:Ian_Maatsen) out injured plus the suspended Joao Gomes — but honestly, even with everyone available, neither side might actually score or win this. Maybe the substitutes could use a change of luck?

**Home/away advantage**: Spurs are at home, and given how little separates the two sides' form right now, home advantage carries extra weight in this read.

**Verdict**: it's genuinely hard to feel confident about this one, but Spurs are at home, and if they can't even bounce back against Villa, they might not have a season worth bouncing back for. So a small lean toward Spurs, hoping they finally break the goal drought.

## Brighton vs Arsenal: behind the seven-game win streak, the defensive depth faces its first real test

**Current form**: Arsenal's form leaves nothing to complain about — 4 wins from 4 in the league for 12 points, and factoring in cup results, they're on a seven-match winning streak across all competitions. Brighton are red-hot too, coming off a 5-0 demolition of Coventry, and with Brighton at home and in high spirits, this isn't an opponent to take lightly.

**Injuries are the real variable here**: [William Saliba](wiki:William_Saliba) remains out injured, and both [Cristhian Mosquera](wiki:Cristhian_Mosquera) and [Ben White](wiki:Ben_White_(footballer)) are doubts — White was subbed off at halftime against Sunderland with a groin issue, and [Jurrien Timber](wiki:Jurriën_Timber), while back from injury, only managed 53 minutes against Sunderland and was left out entirely for the League Cup tie against Ipswich (the manager called it precautionary). This is the first time since Arsenal's hot start that their defensive depth has faced multiple question marks at once — good form doesn't mean you can ignore an injury signal that's building up.

**Opponent's state**: Brighton are in similarly hot form, and although the gap looks big on paper, football has never been just about paper. Arsenal's schedule on multiple fronts also seems to have taken a slight toll on their energy levels lately — their scoring output hasn't quite matched expectations in recent matches.

**Verdict**: same conclusion as always — any Arsenal game this season, I'm backing Arsenal, and I'll keep saying that until injuries or fatigue actually cost them a match. Until that happens, the Gunners remain one of the most trustworthy sides this season.

## Bournemouth vs Liverpool: Iraola returns to his old home ground, Liverpool's attack riddled with injuries

**Current form**: Liverpool's form is poor, only 6 points from 4 games, and the attack has looked sluggish and short of ideas. Bournemouth have taken the lead in all 4 games this season but haven't managed to convert any of them into a win — they've drawn their last three. That "always ahead but can't hold the lead" pattern usually points to a team running out of legs or depth late in games, a trajectory you'd expect to see broken or reversed again.

**Morale and mental state**: this match carries extra weight for Liverpool boss Iraola — his first time returning to his old home ground as an opposing manager since leaving Bournemouth in the summer. That kind of emotional factor is hard to quantify, but it's genuinely worth factoring into the read.

**Injuries are the biggest concern here**: Liverpool's attacking options are riddled with absences — [Hugo Ekitike](wiki:Hugo_Ekitike) is out with a calf issue, [Federico Chiesa](wiki:Federico_Chiesa) is missing with a lower-back problem, and the back line has [Conor Bradley](wiki:Conor_Bradley) and Giovanni Leoni both out with knee injuries, with [Joe Gomez](wiki:Joe_Gomez) a doubt. On top of that, marquee signing Barcola had an injury scare last time out — later confirmed as just cramp, so he should be fine to feature — but his performance that match was as familiar as ever: godlike dribbling, ridiculous shooting, and I genuinely still don't know how that particular effort went wide. Bournemouth aren't exactly healthy either (Julian Araujo, Eli Kroupi, Amine Adli, Veljko Milosavljevic), reportedly all close to returning, but probably still out for this one.

**Verdict**: Liverpool's recent record is actually decent — three wins and a draw across all competitions lately, and even in the league they haven't lost, just failed to win. But Bournemouth, despite being winless in the league, are also 3 draws and 1 loss — no defeats piling up either. These are two teams both good at drawing and both in roughly middling form, so personally I think another draw or a low-scoring match is quite plausible here.

## Man City vs Sunderland: the class gap is wide enough that losing Foden barely matters

**Current form**: City have won all 4 to open the season, 12 points, +6 goal difference, one of the only sides in the league with a perfect record. Sunderland have only 4 points from 4 (1 win, 1 draw, 2 losses), -2 goal difference.

**Injuries**: City are without [Phil Foden](wiki:Phil_Foden) this match due to suspension (red card in the derby) — but honestly, losing Foden might be the least costly absence they could have right now. He's mainly playing out wide this season, still capable of flashes of brilliance, but nowhere near irreplaceable — this former City wonderkid is in a genuinely awkward spot at the club these days. [Jérémy Doku](wiki:Jérémy_Doku), meanwhile, has missed all 4 opening games with a calf injury, though manager Maresca hasn't ruled out him returning to the matchday squad for this one. Sunderland aren't having an easy time either — Reinildo is suspended, and Habib Diarra and Romaine Mundle are both out injured, further thinning their midfield and attacking options.

**Class gap**: even without Foden, City's squad quality still has a wide enough gap over Sunderland's — and besides, it's only Foden who's missing.

**Verdict**: I like City to win comfortably at home. Doku returning to the squad would be a bonus, but even without him, don't forget City won with ten men last time out.

## Fulham vs Man United: a test of morale after the derby loss, and United's away-day injury list isn't light either

**Current form**: United lost the derby 0-1 to City last round, so this is a test of morale for them; Fulham, meanwhile, drew 0-0 away at Liverpool, showing solid defensive discipline, and are seeking their first home win of the season.

**Injuries**: United's absentee list is fairly substantial — [Amad Diallo](wiki:Amad_Diallo), Carlos Baleba, [Matthijs de Ligt](wiki:Matthijs_de_Ligt), [Manuel Ugarte](wiki:Manuel_Ugarte), and Tom Heaton are all out injured, with de Ligt's absence hitting their defensive depth directly and Ugarte's hurting their midfield ball-winning. Fulham's injury situation is relatively light — only Tom Cairney is confirmed out, with [Ryan Sessegnon](wiki:Ryan_Sessegnon) a doubt.

**Structural weakness**: the defensive organization Fulham showed away at Liverpool, combined with United's thinner-than-usual back line without de Ligt, adds up to a real test for United's attack.

**Morale and mental state**: the psychological weight of a post-derby-loss morale test, plus playing away from home, is not something to dismiss lightly for United here.

**Verdict**: confidence is low on this one — United's injury list hits both defense and midfield, and Fulham have shown real defensive discipline, so it's hard to back a comfortable United win here. A cautious narrow win, a draw, or even an upset wouldn't be a surprise.

## A long injury list doesn't guarantee a gloomy conclusion, but it's worth raising your guard

The most interesting thing about this round is how heavily the injuries angle weighs in — Spurs, Villa, Liverpool, and United are all carrying long absentee lists into this round at the same time, which isn't that common this early in a season. A long injury list doesn't necessarily mean a team loses (City are still a comfortable favorite even without Foden), but it's definitely worth raising your guard on these teams' current-form reads — especially Arsenal's match: no matter how good the recent form or how pretty the seven-game streak, three question marks appearing in the back line at once is a signal worth carrying into the next round's framework more than any single scoreline.
