India are touring New Zealand for two Tests. The first is at the Basin Reserve in Wellington, and as I write **New Zealand have made 368 in the first innings, while India are 159/1 after 39 overs, scoring at a rate of 4.07 runs an over**. That is a very efficient pace, and I expect India to finish the first innings on 450 or more and take a lead of 80 to 100. The second Test moves to Hagley Oval in Christchurch, where the conditions are completely different, so **my betting direction for the two matches is opposite**.

---

## 1st Test, Wellington: Three Days in and Still in the First Innings, the Draw Is Likeliest

According to Bet365's in-play probability model, **India are on 38.3%, well ahead of New Zealand on 17.5%, and the draw is at 44.2%**. The match is on day 3 and the first innings is not yet finished, so neither side is likely to have time to bowl the other out twice, and after the first few days' play the pitch has become flatter, which is relatively unhelpful for bowlers. Looking at it that way, **a draw is very likely, and the draw is my pick for this Test**.

Beyond that, two other bets are worth considering: **a combined first-innings total of more than 700 runs** and **a batter scoring a century in an innings**. From past matches, when the side batting first reaches 350, the side batting second has a good chance of also passing 350, and sometimes 400; and with India's batters looking in excellent form at the moment, a century from one of the stronger batters is something I would expect.

## 2nd Test, Christchurch: A Green, Bouncy Ground and the Hosts' Seamers

The second Test is scheduled for **Hagley Oval in Christchurch**, a ground known for a green surface, quick pace and high bounce, which suits fast bowlers. New Zealand have two quick bowlers in **Kyle Jamieson and Matt Henry**, who should have a strong home advantage here. India, though, have **Jasprit Bumrah**, who is also quick, and whose unusual action, with an extremely forward release point, makes the ball feel faster to the batter. **Mohammed Siraj** is a little slower, but is known for his wobble seam, which can use the grass to create unpredictable bounce and take wickets. With a pitch that helps the bowlers, **more than 80% of past Tests at this ground have produced a result within five days**, so a result is far more likely in the second Test than in the first.

**So which side has the edge?** Going by the historical record, **India are at a clear disadvantage at this ground**. New Zealand's win rate here is very high, and in 2020 they beat India here by seven wickets. Christchurch's particular wind and bounce also fit the strengths of New Zealand's bowlers perfectly, and if they win the toss and bowl first, they add the benefit of early-morning moisture. India's batters have more talent and firepower, but against an unfamiliar ground and balls that are harder to read, I doubt they will be able to bring that advantage to bear.

So the second Test should be decided by the bowlers. **My direction is the opposite of the first Test: I expect low scores from the batters, and I lean to New Zealand for the win.**

## Both Calls Lean on the Pitch, and a Test Has Plenty of Room to Surprise

Everything in this article rests on what the surface will do: the draw in the first Test assumes the pitch stays flat and the first innings keeps eating time, and the lean to New Zealand in the second assumes Hagley Oval plays the way it has in the past.
