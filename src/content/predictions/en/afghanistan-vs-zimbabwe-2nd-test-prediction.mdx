Afghanistan and Zimbabwe are playing two Tests in Sharjah. **The first ended in a draw: Zimbabwe 504 and 523/5, Afghanistan 514, with only 25 wickets falling in five days.** Nobody won that match, and that is one of the features of the Test format. For the second Test the odds are **Afghanistan about 2.3, Zimbabwe about 3.0 and the draw about 3.3**; with the margin removed, the three outcomes sit between 28% and 41%, **which means the market itself isn't sure this match will produce a result.** I'll look at it through recent form, the pitch and weather, injuries and squads, bowler fatigue, what the market is saying, head-to-head and specific tactical weaknesses, and **I'm treating the drawn first Test as the most important piece of evidence.**

## Afghanistan vs Zimbabwe, 2nd Test: Can Either Side Bowl the Other Out Twice?

**Start**: Friday 13 November, 10:00 local time in the UAE (06:00 GMT), Sharjah Cricket Stadium, five days. **Odds** (taken 11/12, average of 24 bookmakers, exchanges excluded): Afghanistan 2.28 / Zimbabwe 3.04 / draw 3.25; with the margin removed, about 40.8% / 30.6% / 28.7%.

**Recent form**: the first Test was **a batting battle in which neither side could bowl the other out**: Zimbabwe 504 and 523/5, Afghanistan 514, with Afghanistan leading by only 10 on the first innings. Afghanistan won the earlier T20I series 3-0, but Test cricket and T20Is are different games; Tests test a bowling attack's ability to take wickets much more, and from the result of the first Test, neither attack has that decisive quality. **On pure batting, Zimbabwe have a slight edge, with [Taylor](wiki:Brendan_Taylor) and [Ervine](wiki:Craig_Ervine) the two most experienced players in this series**: Taylor was Player of the Match, and [Bennett](wiki:Brian_Bennett_(cricketer)), Ervine (206\*) and [Madhevere](wiki:Wessly_Madhevere) (his first Test hundred) also made big scores. **Afghanistan have [Rahmat Shah](wiki:Rahmat_Shah) 173, Rahmanullah 113 and [Atal](wiki:Sediqullah_Atal) 105**, three hundreds of their own, so **the batting isn't far apart.**

**Pitch and weather**: the pitch in the first Test was "very flat", the ball wore quickly, and **the seamers got almost no help and the spinners could only bowl defensively**; if the second Test's pitch is prepared along the same lines (as the post-match reports expect), **450 or more in an innings wouldn't be a surprise**, and to get a result in a Test **one side has to bowl the other out in both innings**; judging from the first Test and both bowling attacks, achieving that within 5 days without rain **won't be easy**. Humidity and the sun will make days 4 and 5 more tiring, but won't necessarily make the pitch harder to bat on. The forecast for Sharjah (Open-Meteo, 11/12) shows no rain on any of the five days, with highs around 29 to 30°C, so there's no rain to take time out of the match. ⚠️ I don't know whether the second Test will use the same pitch.

**Injuries and squads**: **[Yamin Ahmadzai](wiki:Yamin_Ahmadzai) didn't play the first Test, and will come in if the pitch "offers more help" (the bowling coach's own words)**; **on the Afghan side, main bowler [Rashid Khan](wiki:Rashid_Khan) and opener [Ibrahim Zadran](wiki:Ibrahim_Zadran) are not in the Test squad: the Afghanistan Cricket Board's squad announcement said they are being rested from Test cricket on medical advice, and neither is in the 15-man squad listed for this match.** On the Zimbabwe side, [Ngarava](wiki:Richard_Ngarava) is out of the series with a back injury and fast bowler Tanaka Chivanga is also out injured; Brian Bennett (22) captains the side, and Graeme Cremer is back in the Test squad. **⚠️ The playing XIs aren't out yet; defer to the official team sheet.**

**Bowler fatigue**: **Afghanistan's [Zia-ur-Rehman](wiki:Zia-ur-Rehman) bowled 96 overs in the first Test**, with [Saleem](wiki:Mohammad_Saleem_(cricketer)) and [Qais Ahmad](wiki:Qais_Ahmad) bowling 43 and 32; **Zimbabwe's Cremer bowled 55 overs (in a single innings)**. **Both attacks have only had three days' rest after a heavy workload.** If the pitch doesn't change, **whose bowlers run out of steam first** is the key to whether the Test is decided on day 3 or 4.

**What the market is saying**: Afghanistan about 40.8%, Zimbabwe about 30.6%, the draw about 28.7% (margin removed). **The draw is only at about 29%, even though the first Test has just been drawn, and drawn with big scores from both sides**; the market leans slightly towards a result, but the gap is small, which shows low confidence. **Coral, Ladbrokes, BetVictor and Paddy Power have the draw at about 3.0 (Betway and Neds at 2.95), while PointsBet and Betr (3.50) and Unibet's group (3.40) are higher, a gap big enough to be worth shopping for the higher price, although the best price has come down from 3.75 on 11/11.**

**Head-to-head**: Afghanistan 2 wins, Zimbabwe 1, 2 draws; **in both 2021 and 2024-25, Afghanistan failed to win the first Test and won the second to take the series**, but both times they had Rashid Khan.

**Depth and tactical weaknesses**: it was clear from the first Test that neither side's batting has much of a problem; the problem is the lack of bowlers who can take wickets and finish the match. **Afghanistan's main bowlers are Zia-ur-Rehman, Qais Ahmad, Saleem and Ziaur Rahman, plus part-timers such as [Shahidi](wiki:Hashmatullah_Shahidi) and Abdul Malik**, but on this dry, hard pitch, the movement their swing bowlers can generate is limited; in the first Test, the Afghan coaching staff tried to take wickets with extra pace and picked the faster Saleem Safi over Yamin Ahmadzai. Judging by the coaches' comments after the match, they'd likely keep that set-up if conditions don't change. **Zimbabwe's attack relies mainly on Cremer (leg spin) plus [Nyamhuri](wiki:Newman_Nyamhuri) and [Muzarabani](wiki:Blessing_Muzarabani)**, with Nyamhuri's 5-82 the standout so far, but again, they lack the ability to finish a match. **You could say the biggest weakness in this match is the same for both sides: on this pitch, neither can take all 20 of the opposition's wickets.**

**Verdict**: **the draw (average odds 3.25, up to 3.50).** Judging from the first Test, I think that unless the pitch changes much, this match is likely to be another draw. **First, only 25 wickets fell in five days of the first Test, and the two sides scored 1,541 runs between them, which shows neither side can reliably take wickets. On the pitch, both coaches independently said there was "nothing in it for the seamers".** With both attacks missing their main bowlers (Rashid out, Ngarava out) and the rest lacking a decisive weapon, **I can't see either side bowling the opposition out twice within 5 days**. Although the head-to-head record favours Afghanistan, they had Rashid Khan both of those times. This time, judging from the odds, I think the draw is a very attractive betting option.

**Note**: in Test cricket a "draw" means that **when the five days end, neither side has managed to bowl the other out twice**; it does not mean the scores finished level.

## Backing the Draw Means Backing "Neither Side Can Finish the Job"

Although on paper Afghanistan may have a slight edge, Test cricket is not T20: even with a slight edge, if you can't bowl the other side out you have to accept a draw. So **backing the draw is mainly backing "neither side can bowl the other out"**. But in turn, I reached this conclusion mainly from the conditions of the first Test; the sample is small, and the argument rests on the pitch rather than on the players' form. If the second Test's pitch is different, the argument may not hold. Betting carries risk, so please bet responsibly.
