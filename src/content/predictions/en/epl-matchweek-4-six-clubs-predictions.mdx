Premier League matchweek 3 is done, and matchweek 4 is up next, headlined by the Manchester derby. Based on squad news and each side's current form, here are my predictions for the focus clubs.

## Man United vs Man City: the Enzo-led midfield trio is clicking, and even the derby at home won't stop City

Whether you look at the odds or current form, City are clearly the more fancied side in this derby. Since [Enzo](wiki:Enzo_Fernández)'s move, City have picked up back-to-back wins in the Premier League and Champions League. The new midfield trio of [Enzo](wiki:Enzo_Fernández), [Cherki](wiki:Rayan_Cherki), and [Anderson](wiki:Elliot_Anderson) genuinely looks like it has quality, linking up well with [Haaland](wiki:Erling_Haaland), who has scored in both of the last two matches.

United, meanwhile, rode a [Bruno Fernandes](wiki:Bruno_Fernandes) hat-trick to a 5-2 win in matchweek 2, but were then held to a draw by Everton in stoppage time in matchweek 3 — they've conceded in three straight matches, and the left-back defensive issue still hasn't been solved. Even at home, I don't think they'll be able to hold off City here. I like City to win this one, and given City's defense is still settling in post-Enzo, both teams to score is also worth considering.

## Sunderland vs Arsenal: the Gunners' squad depth remains the guarantee

I've said it in previous rounds' predictions: any Arsenal match this season, I'm backing Arsenal (though City are now genuinely not to be underestimated either) — their quality and bench depth speak for themselves. Playing on two fronts might bring some fatigue, but against a side at Sunderland's level, getting the points shouldn't be an issue. Given the Gunners have only conceded once all season — to a Chelsea side whose attack has been genuinely ferocious — their defensive record speaks for itself, so Both Teams to Score: No is also worth a look.

## Liverpool vs Fulham: mixed results lately, but a home win shouldn't be an issue

Liverpool have had a bit of both good and bad lately. The good news: they beat Ipswich 2-0 in matchweek 3, then came from behind to beat Atlético Madrid 2-1 in the Champions League — back-to-back wins. The bad news: their marquee signing [Barcola](wiki:Bradley_Barcola), on whom so much hope rests, went off with a calf injury against Atlético and his next appearance is now in doubt. That said, Fulham's season has been considerably worse — winless in three, currently 19th in the league, ahead of Coventry only on a thin goal-difference margin — and with Liverpool at home, taking care of Fulham should be straightforward. On the other hand, given Liverpool's defense still has issues (the goal conceded against Atlético was a clear communication breakdown) and Fulham's attack has also been underwhelming and inefficient in front of goal, the total-goals and BTTS markets are genuinely hard to call — I wouldn't recommend a side bet on either.

## Spurs vs Everton: this isn't a slump, it's a system they haven't adapted to yet — I'm backing Everton here

Spurs at 1.95 and Everton at 3.8 for this one? Even though it's a Spurs home game, and I do think Spurs will eventually turn things around, I don't think it's happening this round. Tottenham's recent struggles aren't a simple form dip — it's clearly that the players haven't adapted to this tactical system yet, and it shows on the pitch. Everton, meanwhile, are only 1-2-2 this season but have scored in all three matches, and their last outing was a dramatic stoppage-time equalizer against United. Whether you look at momentum or recent form, Everton are the side I favor slightly here. That said, given Spurs' squad value and home advantage, my confidence is only moderate — a small stake on Everton is as far as I'd go.

## Chelsea vs Hull City: the promoted side's record has some padding — I like the Blues' firepower at home

Can you believe the current third-place team in the Premier League is promoted side Hull City? They sit 3rd on two wins and a draw, and that opening-day win over Man United was genuinely impressive. But look closer and there's some padding in that record — they didn't actually control possession well in that United game, and while both their goals were well taken, United's shoddy set-piece defending helped plenty too. Their 1-0 win over Coventry doesn't mean much either — Coventry are currently bottom of the table, and Coventry themselves just lost 0-1 to Sunderland in the League Cup — so Hull's form is gradually regressing to the mean. Chelsea, meanwhile, have been in shootouts all season — conceding seven but scoring eight across their matches, genuinely ferocious in attack. Their defense has its concerns, but with that firepower plus home advantage, I still like Chelsea to win this one. Given Chelsea's style of play this season, Over 2.5 goals is also well worth considering.
