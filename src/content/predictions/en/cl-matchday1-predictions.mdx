The Champions League league phase kicks off this week. Based on squad news and each side's current form, here are my predictions for three headline fixtures.

## Napoli vs Arsenal: a clear gap on paper, an away win shouldn't be an issue

Arsenal's start to the season has been about as good as it gets — a Community Shield win over Man City, three straight Premier League wins to open the campaign, one of only a handful of teams still with a perfect record. On the squad front, [Saliba](wiki:William_Saliba) is out with a back injury and [Mosquera](wiki:Cristhian_Mosquera) is missing with a minor muscle knock, but with [Konsa](wiki:Ezri_Konsa) stepping in alongside [Gabriel](wiki:Gabriel_Magalh%C3%A3es), the Gunners' defensive depth looks like it can absorb that fine. Their firepower this season hasn't been quite at the relentless level of Barcelona or Real Madrid, but the attacking line has looked sharp — Saka and Ødegaard both look to be in excellent form, and Havertz showed real influence in the last match against his old club, Chelsea.

Napoli, meanwhile, are having the opposite kind of start — an opening-round away win at Genoa followed by back-to-back losses to Como and Inter Milan, leaving them 12th in Serie A. Their squad situation has only gotten worse: former Man United man [McTominay](wiki:Scott_McTominay) is out after corrective heart surgery, [Anguissa](wiki:Frank_Anguissa) is missing with an injury, and [Neres](wiki:David_Neres) has only been able to train individually. With that clear a gap between the two squads, I don't think Napoli have much chance of holding off an Arsenal side in this kind of form. I like Arsenal to pick up a comfortable away win here.

## Liverpool vs Atlético Madrid: the Reds are unsteady, but home advantage and a new arrival should tip it

Liverpool's start under [Iraola](wiki:Andoni_Iraola) has honestly been underwhelming — back-to-back draws against Newcastle and Nottingham Forest before finally getting their first win of the season, a 2-0 victory at Ipswich powered by [Isak](wiki:Alexander_Isak) scoring twice inside the first nine minutes. What's got everyone's attention this match is the marquee new signing, [Barcola](wiki:Bradley_Barcola) — the winger who won the Champions League twice with PSG and whose move was confirmed right before the transfer deadline — who's expected to make his first Liverpool start here; his dribbling and explosiveness out wide should give the attack a real lift. But Liverpool's concerns this season go beyond attack — the defense has its own question marks. They've conceded two goals in each of their first two league matches, the worst record among the current top six bar Chelsea, and while they finally kept a clean sheet last time out, the issues at right-back haven't gone away — the opponent created several good chances down that side during the match, and getting away with a clean sheet owed something to luck.

Atlético Madrid, for their part, just suffered a 3-0 away loss at Athletic Bilbao that ended their unbeaten start to the season, with manager [Simeone](wiki:Diego_Simeone) calling the defeat "a real wake-up call." On the squad front, [Sørloth](wiki:Alexander_S%C3%B8rloth) is out with a thigh injury, and [Julián Álvarez](wiki:Juli%C3%A1n_%C3%81lvarez) ultimately stayed at the club after a summer full of transfer drama — but how he actually performs on the pitch, given the friction he's reportedly had with the club, and whether it spills over into the dressing room atmosphere, both remain open questions. Looking at recent head-to-head history, Liverpool have won the last three meetings against Atlético, and last season's Champions League league-phase meeting was also a 3-2 comeback win settled by a stoppage-time [Van Dijk](wiki:Virgil_van_Dijk) header. Weighing current form, head-to-head history, and home advantage together, I still lean toward a Liverpool win here, but my confidence is genuinely middling — between Barcola still bedding in and the right-back situation that keeps me uneasy, this one isn't a lock.

## Manchester United vs Sabah: United are struggling, but the gap in Champions League pedigree is enormous

United were just held to a 2-2 draw by Everton and currently sit 11th in the Premier League — that's not "middling," that's genuinely poor form. If Tottenham weren't in even worse shape and soaking up most of the criticism right now, United would probably be getting torn apart. But their opponent here, Sabah from Azerbaijan, are making their first-ever appearance in the Champions League league phase, and there's simply no comparing their level or squad depth to United's. Sabah have been in decent domestic form (a 5-0 home win over Zira last time out), but that's domestic football — it's an entirely different tier from a traditional heavyweight like United. The odds alone tell the story: the market's implied win probability for United sits near 85%, and despite their poor recent form, a home win here shouldn't be in real doubt. Hopefully this result gives them something to rebuild confidence and form around — otherwise United's Premier League season is shaping up to be a genuinely difficult one.
