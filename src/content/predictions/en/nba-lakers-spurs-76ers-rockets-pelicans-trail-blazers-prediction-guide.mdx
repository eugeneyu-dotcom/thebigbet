The catch is that this is the second night of a back-to-back for Philadelphia, on the road in Houston, so they are likely to rest several key stars and it won't be a full-strength matchup. **Pelicans vs Trail Blazers** is a lopsided matchup on paper; does anyone really think New Orleans can win? ## Lakers @ Spurs: Can a Shorthanded San Antonio Backcourt Contain Dončić?

**Tip-off**: 11/28 (Sat) 10:30 Taiwan time / 11/27 (Fri) 21:30 US Eastern, NBA Cup group play. **Lines** (taken 11/27 at 15:07 Taiwan time, Pinnacle): Spurs -6 (1.94) / Lakers +6 (1.94); moneyline Lakers 2.87, Spurs 1.46; total 225 (over 1.93 / under 1.93); with the margin removed, about 66.3% Spurs and 33.7% Lakers. This is the teams' first meeting of the season.

**Recent form**: the Lakers are 15-3 with a +6.7 margin; the Spurs are 15-4 at +11.3. After the Pistons ended their 12-game streak, San Antonio won three more, then lost 111-116 in Portland on 11/25; **the Lakers' last four games were all against weak teams, yet they lost to the Jazz and beat the Kings by only 2**.

**Injuries**: the Spurs are without Harper (18.4 points, hip; ESPN upgraded him on 11/26 from an expected two-to-three-week absence to questionable), and **Fox (right shin contusion) is also questionable**; Wembanyama missed 11/23 with lower-back tightness but was back against Portland (44 points, 16 rebounds). **The Lakers' Reaves (24.7 points, left heel) is likely back**: ESPN lists him as questionable and he practiced on Wednesday, and the Lakers went 5-1 in the six games he missed. **This could be the game where the two teams' injury situations flip: the Lakers are about to get their second scorer back just as the Spurs are missing two guards.** ⚠️ Check the official injury report for whether those three play.

**Rest and travel**: the Lakers have had three days off; the Spurs one (they flew back after playing in Portland on 11/25).

**Home/road**: the Spurs are 7-3 at home and actually 8-1 on the road, and all three home losses came against teams over .500; the Lakers are 7-1 on the road. **Home court hasn't helped San Antonio.**

**NBA Cup**: point differential in group play affects who advances, so both teams *might* have a reason to run up the score, but probably not a strong one; both teams' real targets are the playoffs and the title rather than the NBA Cup.

**What the market is saying**: Pinnacle has the Spurs -6, about 66% with the margin removed, and the day before most other books had -6.5 (a few -5.5). **Even with Harper out and Fox uncertain, the market is laying 6 or more with the Spurs**; the total of 225 sits between the Spurs' average combined score (about 223) and the Lakers' (232.1).

**Depth**: without Harper and Fox, San Antonio's backcourt comes down to Castle, Vassell, Champagnie and McLaughlin. With Reaves back, the Lakers start Dončić, Reaves, Thybulle or Grimes, LaRavia and Kessler, with Sexton, Mamukelashvili and Ziaire Williams off the bench.

**Specific tactical weaknesses**: the Spurs shoot only 33.7% from three; they lost to the Pistons on 11/17 going 9 of 40 from deep and to Portland going 10 of 36, so their three-point shooting has been shaky lately. Beyond Wembanyama they also lack a second rim protector, so when he is pulled out to the perimeter the paint is still exposed. The Lakers turn it over a lot (15.5 a game), which needs watching against a Spurs team that is good at forcing turnovers. And in the games without Reaves, Dončić's ball-handling load clearly went up; without Grimes's explosion (47 points) they might not even have beaten the Hornets. Dončić's usage rate this season is 40.4%, a career high and the third highest in history, and counting the points he creates with his assists he generates about 54 points a game (36.6 points, 7.7 assists), carrying nearly half of the Lakers' offense by himself.

**Key matchups**: Dončić (36.6 points) against Castle; Wembanyama against Kessler; and how Reaves looks on his return.

**Verdict: Spurs moneyline, slight lean to the under.** The market backs the Spurs, but their loss to Portland exposed a number of problems without Harper and Fox. First, the shooting is unreliable: take out Wembanyama and the team shot only 36.7%, and just 25.9% from three, a serious cold spell. Then again, that's a single small sample, and the Lakers' backcourt defense is even worse, so I don't think it will be the norm. Second, the most effective answer to the Lakers so far seems to be the one the Jazz found: send lots of doubles at Dončić, be physical with him and force the ball out of his hands, and with Fox out, Castle and Vassell are ideal defenders with the size and defensive ability for that job. Dončić's shooting has also tended to drop against the Spurs in the past, with his three-point percentage falling under 30%, so unless Reaves comes back and has a dominant game, I think the edge is with San Antonio even shorthanded. **On the total**, for the same reasons, Dončić's shooting dip and the Spurs' recent inconsistency, I lean slightly to the under (225), but without much conviction.

## 76ers @ Rockets: Second Night of a Back-to-Back, How Many Stars Will Philadelphia Have Left?

**Tip-off**: 11/29 (Sun) 09:30 Taiwan time / 11/28 (Sat) 20:30 US Eastern. **Lines**: as of 11/27 at 15:07 Taiwan time, the game hadn't opened on The Odds API. This is the teams' first meeting of the season.

**Recent form**: the 76ers are 4-1 in their last five (including a 7-point win over Miami on 11/25) and lead the East at 15-4; the Rockets are 14-5, 3-2 in their last five (losses to Miami and Denver), and **9-1 at home**.

**Injuries**: Embiid has missed two games with an illness (ESPN lists him as questionable for 11/27 US Eastern against Indiana), but this is the second night of a back-to-back; Brown played only 17 minutes on 11/25 with a left hamstring problem, is already ruled out against Indiana and is getting imaging. ESPN's injury page lists no Rockets players. ⚠️ The official injury report for this game wasn't out when this was written.

**Rest and back-to-back**: the 76ers are on the second night of a back-to-back (at Indiana the night before, an NBA Cup game), while the Rockets have had two days off. **Philadelphia have had three back-to-backs this season, and LeBron sat one game of each, as did Embiid**, so there's a good chance one of them sits this one, maybe both.

**Home/road**: Houston are 9-1 at home; Philadelphia are 7-1 on the road, so it's strength against strength.

**What the market is saying**: not open yet; the line will depend heavily on which of LeBron, Embiid and Brown play.

**Depth**: without Embiid and Brown, plus back-to-back rest, the 76ers could be down to Maxey, Edgecombe and role players; Houston's nine- or ten-man rotation is all available (Durant, Şengün, Amen Thompson, Jabari Smith Jr., VanVleet, Eason, Sheppard, Smart, Adams, Capela).

**Specific tactical weaknesses**: Houston's three-point defense is among the league's best (opponents shoot 32.9%), but the 76ers take only 34.1 threes a game this season and don't rely much on the three anyway. Şengün had a stretch early in the season where his efficiency dropped and he couldn't finish at the rim; he has steadied lately but still has the odd off night. In the paint, the Rockets average 45.3 rebounds, and without Embiid the 76ers were crushed on the boards (33-49 against Portland on 11/23), so if he sits again, rebounding is still a big problem. Philadelphia take good care of the ball (11.4 turnovers a game), several of their stars can create on their own, and their transition and early offense has always been elite; but the offense lacks some team play and can lean too much on isolation.

**Key matchups**: Şengün (21.9 points, 7.0 assists) against Walker and Barlow; Durant against Edgecombe and LeBron; Maxey against VanVleet and Amen Thompson.

**Verdict: Rockets moneyline, slight lean to the under.** The spread isn't out yet, so I can't say what the line will be. But this is the second night of a back-to-back for the 76ers, who are expected to rest some stars, and with Embiid, Brown and even Maxey all having had physical issues recently, the chances of a full-strength lineup are low; Houston, 9-1 at home with two days off, should be at full strength. At full strength on both sides this one would be hard to call, but with Philadelphia expected to rest players, and with the top Western teams looking like the stronger group right now compared with the East, I lean to Houston. On top of that, the 76ers' isolation scoring is unlikely to have much success against Houston's athletic wings led by Amen Thompson, with former Defensive Player of the Year Marcus Smart on the bench as well. **On the total**, Houston play slowly (an estimated 98.7 possessions, the slowest of these six teams) and defend well, and the 76ers are expected to be shorthanded, so I lean slightly to the under, though it depends on the line that opens.

## Pelicans @ Trail Blazers: Why Are the Pelicans So Bad, and Why Are the Blazers So Hot?

**Tip-off**: 11/30 (Mon) 07:00 Taiwan time / 11/29 (Sun) 18:00 US Eastern. **Lines**: as of 11/27 at 15:07 Taiwan time, not open yet. This is the teams' first meeting of the season.

**Recent form**: the Pelicans are 5-14 and 1-8 in their last nine; the Trail Blazers are 12-6, with back-to-back wins over the 76ers by 17 and the Spurs by 5.

**Injuries**: New Orleans are without Murray and Mathurin, Watford may return, and Zion and Murphy III have just come back; Portland are without Morant, Sharpe, Krejčí and Youngblood, **but they have kept winning during Morant's absence** (Morant was on the roster on 11/25 but a DNP).

**Rest and back-to-back**: both teams have had a day off; this is the first night of a back-to-back for New Orleans (at Sacramento the next day).

**Home/road**: New Orleans are 1-6 on the road; Portland have played only six home games this season (4-2) and are only now starting a run at home.

**What the market is saying**: not open yet; Portland should be big favourites, so the size of the spread is the key.

**Depth**: Portland's bench has Jrue Holiday (14.1 points), Henderson and Robert Williams III; New Orleans' bench has Queen and Poole.

**Specific tactical weaknesses**: New Orleans' defense this season has been a disaster (122.5 points allowed a game, opponents shooting 48.6%), with a defensive rating of only 120.2, one of the worst in the league. The offense is a mess too: after Dejounte Murray publicly requested a trade, he isn't expected to play for the Pelicans again; young guard Jeremiah Fears, whom they are developing, has decent-looking numbers but shoots only 28.8% from three, leans too heavily on driving to the rim, and overlaps with Zion Williamson's game, so spacing is a serious problem; and Derik Queen, another key young piece, shoots 37% from three but takes only 1.4 a game, and is undersized and can't protect the rim effectively.

Portland's problems are mainly injuries and the backcourt. Their main star, Avdija, has been red hot this season, averaging 24.9 points and shooting a career-high 43.3% from three, and his ability to both handle and score makes Portland's offense very varied; but he is shooting under 70% from the free-throw line, and several games have got tighter than they should have because of it. Then there's the backcourt, once considered well stocked at the start of the season: after losing Ja Morant and Shaedon Sharpe to injury, the scoring leans too heavily on the 36-year-old Lillard, while Henderson, once highly touted, has been pushed to the margins this season and his shooting has dropped sharply; he has bounced back a little lately, but his game still doesn't fit the current team. Lately, though, Avdija and Lillard have the offense humming, while Clingan and Williams III anchor the defense, both of them real threats as rim protectors and help defenders, which keeps Portland efficient at both ends.

**Key matchups**: Avdija against Herbert Jones; Zion (58.3% shooting) against Clingan and Williams III; and Lillard, shooting 39.4%, whose touch decides how much Portland win by.

**Verdict: Trail Blazers against the spread (if the line is 7 or more, switch to the moneyline).** As I said above, I mainly picked this game to look at why the Pelicans are in such bad shape. There's no doubt I back Portland to win (although the last time I said that, it blew up on me; but that was Pistons vs Spurs, and the gap here is bigger, so surely... not again?). The real question is how many points the spread will be, and **my suggestion is that if Portland are laying 7 or more, just take the moneyline instead**. **On the total**, because of New Orleans' terrible defense I lean toward the over, though again it depends on the line that opens.
