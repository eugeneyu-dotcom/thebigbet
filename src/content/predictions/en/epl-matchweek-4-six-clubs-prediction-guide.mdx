Premier League matchweek 3 is done, matchweek 4 is up next, and the Manchester derby is the headline fixture. Rather than just handing you a conclusion, this breaks down how to read these five focus fixtures — for each one I'll walk through **current form, injuries and squad depth, home/away advantage, and what the odds imply about market sentiment**. It's a framework you can reuse for every matchweek going forward.

## Man United vs Man City: the Enzo-led midfield trio is clicking, and even the derby at home won't stop City

**Start with current form**: since [Enzo](wiki:Enzo_Fernández)'s move, City have picked up back-to-back wins in the Premier League and Champions League — that's the first signal worth reading, a key signing settling in faster than expected usually shows up in results almost immediately. The new midfield trio of [Enzo](wiki:Enzo_Fernández), [Cherki](wiki:Rayan_Cherki), and [Anderson](wiki:Elliot_Anderson) is linking up well with [Haaland](wiki:Erling_Haaland), who has scored in both of the last two matches, which tells you the attacking chemistry has already clicked.

**Then check injuries and squad stability**: United rode a [Bruno Fernandes](wiki:Bruno_Fernandes) hat-trick to a 5-2 win in matchweek 2, but were then held to a draw by Everton in stoppage time in matchweek 3 — they've conceded in three straight matches. A pattern like "wins going forward, leaks going backward" usually points to one specific unsolved position, and here it's the left-back.

**Home advantage, sized correctly**: United are at home, and a derby crowd genuinely narrows a talent gap — but that bump shouldn't be allowed to outweigh a structural defensive problem.

**Verdict**: I like City to win this one, and given City's defense is still settling in post-Enzo, both teams to score is also worth considering.

## Sunderland vs Arsenal: the Gunners' squad depth remains the guarantee

**Current form**: I've said it in previous rounds — any Arsenal match this season, I'm backing Arsenal (though City are now genuinely not to be underestimated either) — the read here is squad depth: when a team plays on two fronts, whether it holds up comes down to whether the bench can step in seamlessly.

**Injuries and squad depth**: Arsenal have only conceded once all season, to a Chelsea side whose attack has been genuinely ferocious — a near-impenetrable defensive record like that is a strong signal on its own.

**Home/away advantage**: there's a real gap in quality between Sunderland and Arsenal, so even with Arsenal away and playing on two fronts, getting the points shouldn't be an issue.

**Verdict**: I like Arsenal to win away, and Both Teams to Score: No is also worth a look.

## Liverpool vs Fulham: mixed results lately, but a home win shouldn't be an issue

**Current form**: Liverpool have had a bit of both good and bad lately — the good news is they beat Ipswich 2-0 in matchweek 3, then came from behind to beat Atlético Madrid 2-1 in the Champions League, back-to-back wins, which signals the squad's overall shape is trending up.

**Injuries are the key variable here**: their marquee signing [Barcola](wiki:Bradley_Barcola), on whom so much hope rests, went off with a calf injury against Atlético and his next appearance is now in doubt — when a key starter's fitness is unclear, the right move in your read is to leave yourself some conservative room rather than going all-in on the prediction.

**The opponent's state**: Fulham's season has been considerably worse — winless in three, currently 19th, ahead of Coventry only on a thin goal-difference margin.

**Home advantage**: Liverpool are at home, so taking care of Fulham should be straightforward.

**Verdict**: that said, given Liverpool's defense still has issues (the goal conceded against Atlético was a clear communication breakdown) and Fulham's attack has been underwhelming and inefficient in front of goal too, total goals and BTTS are genuinely hard to call in this specific situation — "both sides have clear gaps, but pointing in different directions" — so I wouldn't recommend a side bet beyond the main result.

## Spurs vs Everton: this isn't a slump, it's a system they haven't adapted to yet — I'm backing Everton here

**Start with what the odds imply**: Spurs at 1.95 and Everton at 3.8 for this one — the market has clearly installed Spurs as heavy favorites at home, but odds reflect what's "likely," not what's "certain," and that's exactly the gap worth digging into with current form.

**The details of current form matter here**: Tottenham's recent struggles aren't a simple form dip — it's clearly that the players haven't adapted to this tactical system yet, and it shows on the pitch. Telling "bad form" apart from "system mismatch" is a step people often skip when reading form, and the two point to very different trajectories from here. Everton, meanwhile, are only 1-2-2 this season but have scored in all three matches, and their last outing was a dramatic stoppage-time equalizer against United — their momentum is trending up.

**Home advantage**: Spurs' squad value and home advantage are a real edge that shouldn't be ignored.

**Verdict**: whether you look at momentum or recent form, I favor Everton slightly here, though my confidence is only moderate — a small stake on Everton is as far as I'd go.

## Chelsea vs Hull City: the promoted side's record has some padding — I like the Blues' firepower at home

**Current form needs to be unpacked, not just read off the table**: can you believe the current third-place team in the Premier League is promoted side Hull City? They sit 3rd on two wins and a draw, and that opening-day win over Man United was genuinely impressive. But look closer and there's padding in that record — they didn't control possession well in that United game, and while both their goals were well taken, United's shoddy set-piece defending helped plenty too; their 1-0 win over Coventry doesn't mean much either, since Coventry are bottom of the table and just lost 0-1 to Sunderland in the League Cup. This is a key step in reading current form: an eye-catching record doesn't mean a solid process — "the record is gradually regressing to the mean" is itself a predictable trajectory.

**The opponent's state**: Chelsea have been in shootouts all season — conceding seven but scoring eight, genuinely ferocious in attack, though their defense has its concerns.

**Home advantage**: with Chelsea at home too, that attacking edge is amplified further.

**Verdict**: with that firepower plus home advantage, I like Chelsea to win this one, and given Chelsea's style of play this season, Over 2.5 goals is also well worth considering.
