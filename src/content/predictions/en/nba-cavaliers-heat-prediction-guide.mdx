## Cavaliers @ Heat: Can a Cleveland Team Without Mobley Beat an Unsteady Miami Defense?

**Tip-off**: 11/14 (Sat) 09:10 Taiwan time / 11/13 (Fri) 20:10 US Eastern, Kaseya Center. **Lines** (taken 11/13 at 16:56 Taiwan time, Pinnacle): Heat -2.5 (1.98) / Cavaliers +2.5 (1.91); moneyline Cavaliers 2.17, Heat 1.76; total 235.5 (over 1.93 / under 1.93); with the margin removed, about 55.2% Heat and 44.8% Cavaliers.

**Recent form**: the Cavaliers are 6-6 and 2-3 in their last 5 (Thunder -8, 76ers +24, Knicks -12, Hornets -12, Knicks +8), with a +2.1 margin, and they won their last game on the road at New York; the Heat are 5-6 with two straight wins (Kings +6, Jazz +6) and a -4.3 margin. **Both of Miami's recent wins came against teams that were 1-10 and 1-11, and both were by only 6 points.** Both teams have the same "beat the weak, lose to the strong" profile: the Heat are 1-5 against teams above .500 and 4-1 against teams below it; the Cavaliers are 3-5 and 3-1, so Cleveland have been better at hanging with good teams. At 6-6, tonight's Cavaliers sit right between the two groups.

**Injuries**: Cleveland are without **Mobley** (16.9 points and 7.3 rebounds this season and the rim-protection anchor; he fell hard late in the 11/11 win at New York and is out for the first time this season) and [Merrill](wiki:Sam_Merrill) (right elbow, out for the last 3 games); ESPN names [Tyson](wiki:Jaylon_Tyson), [Meleek Thomas](wiki:Meleek_Thomas) and [Hezonja](wiki:Mario_Hezonja) as the players in line for more minutes. On Miami's side, **[Thompson](wiki:Klay_Thompson) (left ankle soreness) and [Larsson](wiki:Pelle_Larsson) (illness) are both questionable, and neither practiced Thursday**, while [Giannis](wiki:Giannis_Antetokounmpo) is probable with a right wrist sprain. Mobley is one half of Cleveland's twin towers with [Allen](wiki:Jarrett_Allen), so without him the interior comes down to Allen, Hezonja, [Watson](wiki:Peyton_Watson) and the like; if both Thompson and Larsson are out for Miami, the perimeter is left to [Wiggins](wiki:Andrew_Wiggins), [Hardaway Jr.](wiki:Tim_Hardaway_Jr.) and Conwell, who have been inconsistent (Wiggins has scored 7, 3, 2, 6 and 13 in his last 5, and Hardaway Jr. did not play against Utah). **⚠️ This is the list put together before the game (from ESPN); apart from Mobley, all four of the other names should be checked against the official injury report, and ESPN isn't clear on whether Merrill can play Friday.**

**Rest and travel**: the Heat played at Utah on 11/10, flew home on 11/11 and rested on 11/12, so this is two days of rest; the Cavaliers played at New York on 11/11 and rested on 11/12, so this is one. **Both teams play a back-to-back tomorrow** (Cleveland are home against Dallas, Miami home against Detroit), and ESPN's note on Thompson mentions he could sit the first game of a back-to-back, though that is ESPN's wording and not an official plan. Rest is Miami's one clear situational edge, but it is only a one-day gap.

**Home/road**: the Heat are 1-3 at home (losses to the Timberwolves, Pistons and Mavericks, with a +20 win over the Pacers) and, oddly, 4-3 on the road; the Cavaliers are 2-4 on the road (losses at Philadelphia, Atlanta, Oklahoma City and Charlotte). Both samples are small, and Miami's home record isn't better than their road record, so this edge isn't obvious.

**What the market is saying**: Heat -2.5, about 55.2% / 44.8% with the margin removed, **with 16 of 30 books at -2.5 and 5 at -1.5**; the total of 235.5 (20 books) is exactly Miami's season average (235.7) and above Cleveland's (230.8). **The line says the market believes Miami have a small home edge, and it has clearly already priced in Mobley's absence.** On record and net rating alone the Heat shouldn't be favorites, so the market is looking at Mobley's absence and home court as the two factors in Miami's favor.

**Depth**: Cleveland have Donovan Mitchell and [Harden](wiki:James_Harden) plus Allen, and Watson, Thomas, Hezonja and Proctor are all usable, so even without Mobley and Merrill, against Miami's rotation of eight to nine (Giannis, [Adebayo](wiki:Bam_Adebayo), Davion Mitchell, Thompson, Larsson, [Portis](wiki:Bobby_Portis), Wiggins, Hardaway Jr., Conwell), Cleveland still hold a slight edge in depth. (Both teams have a Mitchell: [Donovan Mitchell](wiki:Donovan_Mitchell) for Cleveland, [Davion Mitchell](wiki:Davion_Mitchell) for Miami.)

**Specific tactical weaknesses**: **the Heat's defensive problem is the three-point shot** (per the season check: 28th in the league in three-point defense, with opponents shooting 41.3% on corner threes), and the Cavaliers take about 41 threes a game and make about 37%, which is almost aimed at that weak spot. **Cleveland's weakness is rim protection and rebounding without Mobley**: against Charlotte on 11/9 they had only 32 rebounds and lost by 12; with Mobley out, the offensive-rebounding threat from Giannis and Adebayo grows, and Giannis will be harder to stop in the paint.

**Verdict**: **Cavaliers +2.5 (the 2.17 moneyline works too)**. The market likes the Heat tonight, but I'm going against it and backing Cleveland, for three reasons:

1. **Using Harden the right way**: in the first games of the season Cleveland clearly cut Harden's time on the ball and his usage rate (USG%) on purpose, trying to spread the shots around and play more of a team game. But neither the team nor Harden looked comfortable with that, and several times the offense stalled in key moments; in the last game Cleveland seem to have recognized the problem and gave Harden a lot more of the ball, and his 28.2% USG% was a season high. He did find his old form, repeatedly running pick-and-rolls that steadied the team. With Cleveland back to using Harden the right way, I think they can fix the offensive problems they ran into early in the season.
2. **The Heat's three-point defense**: as I've said several times above, Miami have a serious three-point defense problem this season; the season check pointed out that they protect the paint, go under screens often and lack a strong perimeter defender, so they can't contain pull-up threes. Tonight they face Mitchell and Harden, two of the best pull-up three shooters, and it could easily be a repeat of the season opener, when the Heat were shot out of the building by [Edwards](wiki:Anthony_Edwards_(basketball)) and [LaMelo](wiki:LaMelo_Ball) against Minnesota. Mitchell and Harden aren't as young or as explosive as Edwards and LaMelo, but they have far more experience, and the Heat will struggle to contain this backcourt pair.
3. **The Heat's inconsistency**: apart from Giannis and Davion Mitchell, nobody on the Heat has been steady this season, and both Adebayo and Thompson have repeatedly shot under 20% in a game. Cleveland may be without Mobley, but they still have Allen to guard Giannis, and given Adebayo's offense this season, he will struggle to punish the paint without Mobley.

All in all, unless Thompson turns in another miracle performance, I think the edge is with Cleveland. **On the total, I lean slightly to the under** (235.5, under 1.93), because of the Heat's inconsistency and because losing Mobley will cost Cleveland something in finishing around the rim.

## Both Teams Are Unsteady, and Even the Market Only Dares to Lay 2.5

Both teams have had disappointing starts, and although each seems to be adjusting slowly, Miami's adjustment looks bumpier than Cleveland's. The teams the Heat have beaten are mostly below .500, and against good teams they still don't have a chance; Cleveland also started poorly, but in the last game they changed course, went back to the style Harden knows and beat the defending champion Knicks, and overall they look to have recovered better than Miami. But both teams are unsteady right now, and you can see it in how close the market's spread is, so betting carries risk; please bet responsibly.
