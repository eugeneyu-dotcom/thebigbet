The Champions League league phase kicks off this week. This piece breaks down three headline fixtures the same way as usual — through **current form, injuries and squad depth, and home/away advantage** — so you can see how to read them, rather than just betting on name value or past reputation.

## Napoli vs Arsenal: a clear gap on paper, an away win shouldn't be an issue

**Current form**: Arsenal's start to the season has been about as good as it gets — a Community Shield win over Man City, three straight Premier League wins to open the campaign, one of only a handful of teams still with a perfect record. That's the most direct signal worth reading here. Napoli, meanwhile, are having the opposite kind of start — an opening-round away win at Genoa followed by back-to-back losses to Como and Inter Milan, leaving them 12th in Serie A. The two sides' form is heading in completely opposite directions.

**Injuries and squad depth**: [Saliba](wiki:William_Saliba) is out with a back injury and [Mosquera](wiki:Cristhian_Mosquera) is missing with a minor muscle knock, but with [Konsa](wiki:Ezri_Konsa) stepping in alongside [Gabriel](wiki:Gabriel_Magalh%C3%A3es), Arsenal's defensive depth looks like it can absorb that fine — that's the key thing to read in an injury situation: not just who's missing, but whether the deputy can slot in seamlessly. Napoli have had no such luck: former Man United man [McTominay](wiki:Scott_McTominay) is out after corrective heart surgery, [Anguissa](wiki:Frank_Anguissa) is missing with an injury, and [Neres](wiki:David_Neres) has only been able to train individually — when multiple injuries hit at once, a squad without real depth struggles to hold up.

**Home/away advantage**: Arsenal are away here, but the gap in squad quality is already large enough that home/away barely factors into the conclusion.

**Verdict**: with that clear a gap between the two squads, Napoli don't have much chance of holding off an Arsenal side in this kind of form. I like Arsenal to pick up a comfortable away win here.

## Liverpool vs Atlético Madrid: the Reds are unsteady, but home advantage and a new arrival should tip it

**Current form**: Liverpool's start under [Iraola](wiki:Andoni_Iraola) has honestly been underwhelming — back-to-back draws against Newcastle and Nottingham Forest before finally getting their first win of the season, a 2-0 victory at Ipswich powered by [Isak](wiki:Alexander_Isak) scoring twice inside the first nine minutes. Atlético, for their part, just suffered a 3-0 away loss at Athletic Bilbao that ended their unbeaten start to the season, with manager [Simeone](wiki:Diego_Simeone) calling the defeat "a real wake-up call" — neither side's form is exactly settled, which means it's worth digging into injuries and squad detail rather than judging off one result alone.

**Injuries and squad depth**: what's got everyone's attention this match is the marquee new signing, [Barcola](wiki:Bradley_Barcola) — the winger who won the Champions League twice with PSG, whose move was confirmed right before the transfer deadline, and who's expected to make his first Liverpool start here; his dribbling and explosiveness out wide should give the attack a real lift. But Liverpool's concerns go beyond attack — the defense has its own question marks. They've conceded two goals in each of their first two league matches, the worst record among the current top six bar Chelsea, and while they finally kept a clean sheet last time out, the issues at right-back haven't gone away — getting away with a clean sheet owed something to luck. On Atlético's side, [Sørloth](wiki:Alexander_S%C3%B8rloth) is out with a thigh injury, and [Julián Álvarez](wiki:Juli%C3%A1n_%C3%81lvarez) ultimately stayed at the club after a summer full of transfer drama — but how he performs given the friction he's reportedly had with the club, and whether it spills into the dressing room atmosphere, both remain open questions. That "stayed, but the tension might not be resolved" situation is exactly the kind of variable the numbers can't show — it just has to be watched.

**Home/away advantage**: Liverpool are at home, and looking at recent head-to-head history, they've won the last three meetings against Atlético — last season's Champions League league-phase meeting was also a 3-2 comeback win settled by a stoppage-time [Van Dijk](wiki:Virgil_van_Dijk) header. Home advantage plus a head-to-head edge is a genuine plus in this read.

**Verdict**: weighing current form, head-to-head history, and home advantage together, I still lean toward a Liverpool win here, but my confidence is genuinely middling — between Barcola still bedding in and the right-back situation that keeps me uneasy, this one isn't a lock.

## Manchester United vs Sabah: United are struggling, but the gap in Champions League pedigree is enormous

**Current form**: United were just held to a 2-2 draw by Everton and currently sit 11th in the Premier League — that's not "middling," that's genuinely poor form. If Tottenham weren't in even worse shape and soaking up most of the criticism right now, United would probably be getting torn apart.

**The gap in class**: their opponent here, Sabah from Azerbaijan, are making their first-ever appearance in the Champions League league phase, and there's simply no comparing their level or squad depth to United's. Sabah have been in decent domestic form (a 5-0 home win over Zira last time out), but that's domestic football — an entirely different tier from a traditional heavyweight like United. When the gap in class is this large, even one side's poor recent form isn't enough to seriously sway the read.

**What the odds imply about market sentiment**: the odds alone tell the story — the market's implied win probability for United sits near 85%, which tells you the market also sees the class gap, not United's recent slump, as the dominant factor here.

**Verdict**: despite their poor recent form, a home win here shouldn't be in real doubt. Hopefully this result gives them something to rebuild confidence and form around — otherwise United's Premier League season is shaping up to be a genuinely difficult one.

## Where the numbers can't reach: whether a grudge is actually settled

The [Álvarez](wiki:Juli%C3%A1n_%C3%81lvarez) situation in the Liverpool match above is the best reminder in this piece — a player staying at a club doesn't mean the friction is actually resolved. That kind of dressing-room atmosphere and mental-state variable doesn't show up in current form or in the odds, no matter how you read them; you only find out once the match is actually played. And then there's a team like Sabah, making their Champions League debut — the gap on paper is enormous, but the thrill of a first-ever appearance on that stage can sometimes push a side to punch above its weight.
