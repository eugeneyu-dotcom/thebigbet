## Heat @ Jazz: Winning Isn't the Hard Part, Winning by 9 Is

**Tip-off**: 11/11 (Wed) 10:10 Taiwan time / 11/10 (Tue) 21:10 US Eastern, Delta Center. **Lines** (taken the evening of 11/10, Pinnacle): Heat -9 (1.96) / Jazz +9 (1.93); moneyline Heat 1.29, Jazz 3.85; total 228.5 (over 1.96 / under 1.91); with the margin removed, about 74.9% Heat and 25.1% Jazz.

**Recent form**: the Heat are 4-6 and 2-1 in their last 3 (Clippers +1, Warriors -20, Kings +6), with a -5.3 margin; the Jazz are 1-9 and **have lost eight straight**, with a -11.7 margin, and **in the 7 games without Jackson they've lost every one, losing by an average of 11.3 over the last 3 (-7, -12, -15)**. We only recently analysed the Heat's form, and several of their numbers sit in the bottom of the league, so they aren't guaranteed to beat weak teams either. But Utah are now without key man JJJ, and their leading star [Markkanen](wiki:Lauri_Markkanen) is in doubt, so the frontcourt is a worry; will the Heat take advantage?

**Injuries**: Utah are missing **George (the team's top ball handler for assists and scoring, with a high of 39 points over his last 10 games), Jackson (torn MCL) and [Nurkic](wiki:Jusuf_Nurkić)**, and **Markkanen is still questionable**; the starting lineup will have to lean on rookies [Peterson](wiki:Darryn_Peterson) and [Bailey](wiki:Ace_Bailey_(basketball)) together with [Hayes](wiki:Jaxson_Hayes) and [Collier](wiki:Isaiah_Collier). Miami only have [Giannis](wiki:Giannis_Antetokounmpo)' wrist as probable, and **he has played more than 30 minutes in 8 of his 10 games this season, 31 and 34 in the last two, so whether the wrist affects his minutes is something to check in the pre-game report**; [Wiggins](wiki:Andrew_Wiggins) moving to the bench and [Larsson](wiki:Pelle_Larsson) moving into the starting five is also a lineup change worth watching. **⚠️ This is the pre-game list (from ESPN); defer to the official injury report.**

**Home/road**: the Heat are 3-3 on the road (better than their 1-3 at home); the Jazz are 1-4 at home (their only win was a one-point squeaker over the Pelicans) and 0-5 on the road. But given how poor both records are, there isn't much to read into home and road.

**What the market is saying**: Heat -9, about 74.9% / 25.1% with the margin removed, **with books split between -9 and -9.5** (19 at -9.5, 5 at -9); the total of 228.5 is above the Jazz's home average (227.8) and their season average (224.1), **but below the Heat's season average (236.9) and their road average (233.5)**. The market seems to be setting the total at "middle to high" for the two teams. **Basically the line says it believes the Heat will win, and the only question is by how much.**

**Depth**: Miami have a stable rotation of 8 to 9 players (Giannis, [Adebayo](wiki:Bam_Adebayo), [Mitchell](wiki:Davion_Mitchell), [Thompson](wiki:Klay_Thompson), Larsson, [Portis](wiki:Bobby_Portis), Hardaway Jr., Wiggins), **but overall they lack a top-class second ball handler** (as the season check noted); with George and Jackson out, and Markkanen in doubt, Utah can't be said to have a bench right now, and even the starting five is a problem.

**Specific tactical weaknesses**: **the Heat's defensive problem is giving up threes** (per the season check: 28th in the league in three-point defense, with opponents hitting 41.3% of corner threes); **the Jazz haven't shot well from three over these 10 games** (34, 37, 29, 30, 38, 28, 35, 23, 38, 29, averaging about 32%), **so if Utah's touch from deep stays cold tonight, the Heat's biggest defensive flaw won't be exploited**. Going the other way, **Utah's ball-handling and organization problem is worse without George**: right now they can only rely on rookie Peterson to carry the backcourt, and although he has shown good potential at the start of the season, he is still a rookie and has some way to go before he is a qualified NBA starter. With their stars out, his ability to carry the load himself is still unproven.

**Verdict**: **Heat moneyline (odds 1.29), and I like the -9 to cover (1.96)**. Even at full strength the Jazz are probably only a team on the edge of the playoffs, with too many young players still developing and a team that hasn't yet gelled. And with so many players out, if George, JJJ and Markkanen all sit, it's very hard for Peterson alone to beat an NBA-level team. So backing the Heat to win is a given; the only question is whether they can clear the 9-point line. My view is that the chance is high, for two main reasons: 1. Utah's frontcourt is thin right now, and both Giannis and Adebayo can pretty much do what they want against it. 2. The Heat's pace is second in the league, and with Utah short of a floor general, there's a good chance Miami push the tempo and turn this into a high-scoring game. All told I think there's a 70% chance of covering, with the remaining 30% being Thompson rolling a 1, and the benches padding the score in garbage time.

## Calling the Winner Isn't the Same as Calling the Line

I can say with a lot of confidence that the Heat will win this game, but over their last 10 games this season they've won by 10 or more only twice, and against opponents at 2-7 and 1-9 they won by 6 or fewer, so winning by 9 is a real challenge. Although I judge from the personnel and the pace that the chance of covering is good, there are still plenty of factors I can't control. Betting carries risk, so please bet responsibly.
