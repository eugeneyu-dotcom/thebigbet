Most NBA teams have now played around ten games, enough for some first real data to check how each is doing. This article is about the Miami Heat, who this summer were willing to sell off several rotation players and draft picks to land former MVP Giannis in the prime of his career. Seen before the season as a playoff team or even a title contender, they have so far managed only a 4-6 record, eighth in the East. That is of course a long way from expectations, and the offensive problems were something everyone saw coming before the season: neither of the two bigs has a reliable perimeter game, so the spacing was always going to be cramped. What nobody expected is that their biggest problem right now isn't the offense but the defense: through ten games their defensive rating is 116.8, only 25th in the league, which is unbelievable for a team with [Giannis Antetokounmpo](wiki:Giannis_Antetokounmpo), [Bam Adebayo](wiki:Bam_Adebayo) and [Andrew Wiggins](wiki:Andrew_Wiggins). Let's take a look at what exactly is going wrong for the Heat.

First, naturally, the defense, their biggest problem right now. The only area where they defend reasonably well is the non-restricted-area paint (In The Paint-Non RA), where opponents shoot 44.5%, 14th in the league; in every other zone they rank 20th or worse. The biggest problem is their three-point defense: opponents are shooting 41.0% against them, only 28th in the league. And behind that number is a defensive scheme that prioritizes the paint and goes under screens a great deal.

In their opening game against the Timberwolves, [Edwards](wiki:Anthony_Edwards_(basketball)) hit nearly nine threes from the perimeter, more by himself than the entire Heat team made. Watching that game, you can see that when the Heat face screens they don't fully switch, the way many teams do these days, nor do they have the screener's defender hedge and recover to shut down the three; they have the on-ball defender go straight under. But with a smaller player like [Mitchell](wiki:Davion_Mitchell) who finds it hard to fight through screens, and [Thompson](wiki:Klay_Thompson), whose speed has declined with injuries and age, in the lineup, they are easily left behind and punished directly by opponents who can shoot threes off the dribble. Making things worse still, Wiggins, the perimeter lead defender they have relied on in the past, has declined across the board this year, with all sorts of instances of being beaten off the first step, which leaves them without a reliable primary defender on the perimeter and forces the two bigs to help much more often, which in turn leaves open corner looks whenever the opponent swings the ball; that is where the 41.3% corner-three percentage opponents are shooting against the Heat comes from. Finally, the Heat's pace this year is very fast, 102.6, second in the league, but the price of a high pace is that they are more easily punished with transition and early offense, and the Heat currently lack defenders who can get back. As a result, 35% of the shots opponents take at the rim come from easy transition and early-offense scoring, and even with two bigs their opponents are shooting 67.8% in the restricted area, a dismal mark that ranks 22nd in the league.

Now the offense. Before the season we knew neither big was reliable from the perimeter and that spacing would be an issue, but we never expected the problem to be this serious. Adebayo looks as if he has forgotten how to play offense, and can't even figure out his off-ball movement, on several occasions cutting at the wrong time and blocking Giannis' driving lane, or dragging his defender onto Giannis' path. That leaves the Heat almost playing four-on-five on offense whenever the two bigs share the floor: one of Adebayo and Antetokounmpo is always standing around watching, or can only fire up the threes opponents are leaving open, and both are shooting under 30% from three. Beyond those two, the rest of the Heat shoot around 35% from three, but the problem is that it's too up and down, with someone often going cold; Thompson and Wiggins have both already had several games under 30% from three this season, and the overall spacing keeps getting tougher. On top of that, with Wiggins having a terrible start, the team doesn't have a reliable second ball-handler to share Antetokounmpo's playmaking load, and in the last few games Coach [Spo](wiki:Erik_Spoelstra) has even been forced to use Thompson as a ball-handler and has deliberately increased the share of pick-and-roll. Over the past two seasons, the Heat's ball-handler pick-and-roll possessions made up only about 15% of their total, one of the lowest rates in the league, with lots of handoffs and cuts instead, in a movement-based Motion system. But in the game against the Hawks this season they ran 57 pick-and-rolls in a single game, a full 10 more than their highest game last season. That shows the Heat really are being forced to start their offense in ways they haven't been good at in recent seasons, but whether this change is a good thing or a bad one remains to be seen.

All in all, the Heat are currently in the growing pains of an offensive system change, while the defense they have always relied on has collapsed completely, so they can't impose themselves at either end of the floor. To improve things, besides hoping Wiggins bounces back, they may first have to change their defensive system, putting more emphasis on contesting opponents' threes and making full use of the two bigs' ability to help inside the arc.
